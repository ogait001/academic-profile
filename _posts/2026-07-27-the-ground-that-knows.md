## I. The Debt

The two essays before this one argued, from opposite directions, that divine knowledge cannot be understood as observation, as reception, or as attention enlarged without limit across a field of objects. "Under the Fig Tree" showed it by exhibiting Christ present to a man's unwitnessed interior; "The Eyes He Does Not Need" showed it by taking apart every finite model of knowing until none remained. The vocabulary those essays established -- externalization, the Wall, reception, condensation, the Crossing Point -- is assumed here rather than rebuilt. What matters for the present essay is only their shared result: God is not a knower set over against what He knows, and every attempt to make Him one, however magnified, smuggles back the separation the divine case does not contain.

That result is entirely negative, and a negative result leaves a debt. To say at such length what divine knowing is not is to incur the obligation of eventually saying what it is, or else to concede that the corpus has only ever gestured at a mystery it cannot state. The restraint was proper -- a positive account offered too early is almost always the discarded finite model creeping back in its Sunday clothes -- but restraint indefinitely prolonged becomes evasion. This essay is written to pay the debt. The task is no longer to remove false images but to identify the one relation that made every previous negation necessary. The surprising thing is that nothing new has to be imported to pay it. The positive account was already lying inside the negative one, in a single relation both prior essays kept reaching for and naming only in passing. That relation is grounding, and the whole of this essay is the claim that grounding, rightly understood, simply is the knowing the other essays could only circle.

<br>

## II. Why the Positive Account Cannot Be Reception Perfected

The first temptation, once negation is set aside, is to reach for the nearest available model of knowing and purify it. We know by reception; perhaps God knows by reception without the defects -- reception without a medium, without delay, without the loss that every finite channel imposes. This is the most natural move available, and it must be refused first, because it is the counterfeit wearing its most convincing face.

Reception is not one feature of finite knowing among others but its whole structure: to know by reception is to be an interior at which something arrives from beyond itself, to stand by definition in the position of the one to whom the world must come. That is the position the finite knower cannot leave, and it is the position, not any weakness within it, that the divine case excludes.

A God who knew by reception, however perfected, would therefore still be an interior to which the world arrives. He would still be the terminus of a crossing, still positioned as the one to whom being must come, still on the far side of a gap, only a gap made infinitesimally thin. Perfecting reception does not exit the family of finite knowers. It produces the family's ideal member, the limit toward which every finite knower aspires and never reaches, which is a different thing entirely from a knower who was never in the family. God is the latter, not the former. Reception perfected is still reception, and reception is still the mark of the finite.

So the positive account cannot be reception's fulfillment. It must be reception's opposite pole. Not the interior to which the world arrives, but the ground in which the arriving occurs -- the ground for which there is nothing to receive, because nothing was ever exterior to it in the first place. And a name for that ground has been in use all along, without its being noticed that the name for God's relation to the creature's moment was also the answer to the question of divine knowledge. The name is sustaining. The ground is the one who holds the Now open.

<br>

## III. The Now Held Open

One phrase keeps returning at the decisive moment, and it says something about being rather than about knowing: that the Now in which anything occurs is not sustained by anything other than God, and that there is no distance between sustaining a moment and being present to what occurs within it. The point has been made from the side of the thought under the fig tree and from the side of the finite model's collapse, and in both the same thing happened -- a question about knowledge was answered by pointing at a relation of being, the relation by which the creature's present moment depends, for its very actuality, on a ground that does not itself occur within any moment.

This is the relation the whole corpus has been circling, and it is worth stating with full weight. The creature does not sustain its own Now. The present in which a person stands -- the crossing point where memory's loop and anticipation's loop meet, where the will faces one way or another, where reception becomes condensation -- is not self-supporting. It is held in being. Nothing in the creature accounts for the sheer actuality of its own present instant; that actuality is given, continuously, by a ground that is not itself a moment and does not itself pass. This is not a poetic embellishment of ordinary temporality. It is the claim that the Now is a dependent actuality, and that what it depends on is God, understood not as a cause that acted once at the beginning but as the standing condition of there being any present at all for the creature to occupy.

There is a sentence, already written in this corpus, that names the whole of it without pausing on what it had said: that there is no distance between sustaining a moment and being present to what occurs within it. That sentence is not a corollary. It is the entire positive thesis, stated as if in passing. If there is no distance between sustaining the Now and being present to its content, then the presence that constitutes divine knowledge is not a second thing God does after sustaining. To sustain the Now is already to be present to everything in it, because being present to its content is not an act performed upon an already-sustained moment from some further position -- there is no further position. The sustaining and the presence are not two relations that happen to coincide. They are one relation, named twice, and the whole of what follows is the drawing out of that single fact.

<br>

## IV. One Act, Named Twice

Here is the thesis, stated flatly and then defended. For God, to sustain the Now at which a reception occurs and to know that reception are not two acts but one act, named twice. It is called grounding when we attend to the fact that the creature's moment depends on it for being. It is called knowing when we attend to the fact that this same dependence leaves no content of the moment exterior to its ground. There is one relation between God and the creature's present, and grounding and knowing are its two faces, distinguished by us and not in Him.

The form of this claim is not new to the corpus; only its object is. The same topology already treats reception and condensation not as two events joined by a delay but as two names for a single occurrence, read from two angles -- reception insofar as something new is given, condensation insofar as that given thing has settled into what the creature is. The present claim makes the identical move one level higher. Grounding and knowing are not two acts joined by any relation at all; they are one act, read from two angles -- grounding insofar as the moment is held in being, knowing insofar as nothing in the moment so held stands outside the ground that holds it. The machine built to dissolve the delay between reception and condensation dissolves, unchanged, the more fundamental delay we imagine between God's being present to a moment and God's knowing what is in it.

Why must these be one act rather than two? Because a knowing that were a second act -- added to the sustaining, directed upon the sustained moment as upon an object -- would reintroduce exactly the gap already removed. If God first sustains the Now and then, in a further act, knows its contents, then between the sustaining and the knowing there stands the very structure of a knower turning toward an object that lies over against it. That structure is reception. A second act of knowing, however immediate, would be God receiving the content of the moment He sustains, and a receiving God is a God returned to the family of finite knowers. The only way to keep divine knowledge outside that family is to deny that it is a second act at all. It is not that God sustains the moment and also, wonderfully, knows it. It is that *the sustaining is the knowing*, because to hold a moment in being with nothing of it outside your holding simply is to know it, in the only sense of knowing that does not reduce to reception.

We can now see, in ordinary words, what has happened. The question was never how God manages to reach a knowledge of things laid out at a distance from Him. It was whether He is at a distance from them at all, and the answer has turned out to be that He is not, because their very being is His gift to them at every instant. Once that is granted, His knowing them is not a further accomplishment requiring explanation; it is simply what it means to give a thing its being with nothing of the thing left over outside the giving. The reason the negations could only say what this knowing is not is that they were looking for it in the wrong category -- among acts of cognition, where every candidate turned out to be a disguised reception -- when it was never a cognitive act laid over the world but the world's dependence, seen from the side of what depends. That is the shift this section has made, and the next will show that it pays for every negation the corpus has been carrying.

This is why the absence of eyes was never a limitation. An eye is the organ by which a knower performs the second act -- turns toward an object, receives what it releases, constructs an interior image of an exterior thing. God performs no second epistemic act, no act directed toward an already constituted object. There is nothing for an organ to do, because there is no object standing over against Him from which content must be gathered. The content of the creature's Now is not gathered by God; it is held by God, and holding-in-being with nothing outside the hold is not a deficient mode of seeing but a mode of knowing to which seeing is not even the impoverished approximation. Seeing belongs to a different family. It is not a lesser grounding. It is the thing grounding is not.

<br>

## V. What This Rescues From Mere Negation

It is worth pausing to see how much negative work is converted, by this single identity, into positive content. The negations were not wrong. They were incomplete, and the identity completes them by giving each one a reason rooted in what divine knowledge is rather than only in what it is not.

God does not know by observation -- because observation is a second act, a turning-toward, and there is no second act; there is only grounding, of which knowing is the face. He does not know "at once" -- because "at once" measures attention against a field of objects, and grounding distributes nothing: it is wholly present to each moment without being divided among them, being given rather than attention parcelled out. He does not know future condensations -- because grounding holds the Now, and the future is not a Now being held but the horizon of receptions the creature has not yet reached, present to the ground as moments it will hold, in a "will" that belongs to the creature's sequence and not to the timeless holding. He does not know by inspecting a timeline -- because a timeline is what condensation produces from inside sequence, and grounding does not stand outside sequence to view it but underlies each moment of it, holding each Now as Now, never as a frame in a finished strip.

Each negation, read through the identity, stops being a mere refusal and becomes a consequence. This is the difference between an apophaticism that has run out of things to say and a positive claim that happens to generate negations as its shadow. The negative essays gave the shadow. This one gives the body that casts it. And the test of whether the body is real is exactly this: that the negations follow from it rather than standing on their own. They do follow. The single identity -- grounding is knowing -- entails every "not" that had to be asserted before it.

## VI. The Objection This Was Always Going to Face

There is a cost to grounding divine knowledge in the sustaining relation, and it must be paid openly, because a reader who has followed the corpus this far is primed to hear the wrong thing. If God knows the creature's interior because that interior's very moment is held in His being, with nothing of it exterior to Him, then it will sound as though the creature's being is inside God's being -- as though the interior known is a region of the divine interior, and the distinction between Creator and creature has quietly dissolved into a single substance with the creature as one of its folds. This is pantheism, and every sentence in this corpus about the Now being "held open by" or "not outside" or "sustained by" God has been walking toward the edge of it. The objection is not a misreading. It is the natural reading, and it must be answered at the root rather than waved off.

The answer is that the relation which does the knowing is not identity but dependence, and dependence is asymmetric in exactly the way identity is not. To say that the creature's Now is held in God's being is not to say that the creature's Now is a part of God's being. It is to say that the creature's being is caused -- continuously, at every instant, in its very actuality -- by God, and that a being so caused has nothing of itself standing outside its cause, precisely because it does not stand on its own at all. The creature is not a fold in God. It is a dependent actuality, other than God by the whole distance between the caused and the uncaused, and yet with no content exterior to its cause, because it possesses no being except the being it is given. These two things are not in tension; they are the same fact. What is wholly dependent is wholly other -- it is not its own ground -- and for that very reason it keeps nothing back from its ground, since it has nothing that is its own to keep.

This is why the knowing that is grounding does not collapse into pantheism, while a knowing that were reception, oddly, would come closer to requiring it. Reception needs the known to be, in some measure, of the same order as the knower -- something that can arrive, that can be taken in, that can become content within the receiver. The identity proposed here needs the opposite. It needs the creature to be radically other than God, caused rather than shared, so that the relation between them is creation and not composition. Presence, here, is not the presence of a part within a whole. It is the presence of the caused to its cause, which is the most asymmetric relation there is. The distinction between Creator and creature is not endangered by this account. It is what the account runs on. Remove it -- make the creature share God's being rather than depend on it -- and the knowing that is grounding becomes incoherent, because there would no longer be a creature whose moment God grounds, only God relating to Himself.

## VII. In What Light We See

The Psalm set at the head of this essay says more than devotion usually hears in it. In Your light we shall see light. It does not say that God provides illumination by which we then, on our own, perform the act of seeing, as a lamp lets an eye do its independent work. It says the seeing happens in His light -- that the very act by which anything is seen occurs within a light that is not the seer's own and not directed from outside upon an object, but the medium and ground in which seeing and seen are together held. Read as a claim about creaturely knowledge, it is beautiful. Read as a claim about divine knowledge, it is exact. God does not shine a light upon the creature's Now in order to see what is there. He is the light in which the Now is, and to be the light in which a moment stands is to have nothing of that moment fall in shadow, not because the illumination is thorough but because there is no exterior source doing any illuminating. The moment does not receive God's knowing as light received from a lamp. It exists in it.

This is the final sense in which divine knowing is not a case of seeing the interior from very close, rather than very far. It is not seeing at all, because seeing is the act of a light directed from a source toward an object across the space between them, and God is not a source across a space. He is the light in which the space, and the object, and the moment they occupy, are held in being. What "Under the Fig Tree" showed happening under a tree -- a man discovering that his most private Now was never his alone -- was not the miracle of a very powerful gaze reaching a very hidden place. It was the ordinary and total fact of every moment's dependence, become visible for once in a single sentence to a single startled man. Nathanael's fig tree was not seen into. It was held open, as every Now is held open, by the one in whose light it occurred.

One boundary should be marked before closing, lest the account claim more than it has earned. What has been shown is that divine knowing is one face of the relation by which the creature is grounded. The classical tradition holds that in God knowing, willing, and loving are likewise not distinct acts but one simple actuality, and the architecture assembled here plainly leans toward that unity. But grounding was shown to be knowing by a specific route -- that to hold a moment in being with nothing outside the hold simply is to know it -- and that route does not, by itself, deliver willing or loving, each of which would require its own derivation rather than inheriting this one. That God's grounding is also His loving may well be true, and may well follow from this same topology; it is deliberately not argued here, and its absence is a restraint, not an oversight. This essay claims one identity and leaves the others to be earned on their own ground.

What remains to be said, the corpus has now said. Divine knowledge is not a faculty God has. It is the intelligible face of the relation by which the creature is held in being -- grounding read from the side of its content, knowing read from the side of its ground, one act throughout, named twice by us because we, who know only by reception, have no single word for a knowing that is not a receiving but a giving of the very being it knows. *The creature knows by taking the world in. God knows by holding the creature in being.* Between those two there is no common measure, and the whole of this corpus has been the slow discovery that there was never meant to be one.

*In lumine tuo videbimus lumen. In Your light we shall see light. Not by it. In it.*
