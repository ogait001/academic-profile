## Abstract

[*When Is the Present?*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/ontology/philosophical%20theology/catholic%20philosophy/thomism/2026/07/03/when-is-the-present.html) gave the future a definition and stopped where the definition was secure: the future is the horizon of receptions not yet actualized, real as openness and not as a region, decided at the crossing by three co-determinants -- the traces a being brings, the world's uninvited givens, and the response of the will. This essay does not reopen that account; it takes it as settled and asks the question that section left unasked. If the future is horizon and not place, what is the structure of a horizon -- and why is it that no one has ever stood in the future, not once, though everyone has always been about to? The answer proposed here is that the future is not merely unentered but unenterable in principle: it recedes at exactly the rate the present advances, so that to reach it is nothing other than to convert it into a Now, at which instant it is no longer future. Where [*What Is the Past?*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/metaphysics/philosophy-of-memory/philosophical-theology/2026/08/17/what-is-the-past-en.html) identifies closure as the operator of pastness, this essay identifies asymptote as the corresponding operator of futurity. The future is asymptotic. One second hence and one decade hence are the same horizon at different scales, because neither is ever occupied as future; both are only ever faced. This yields, finally, a reading of the oldest and hardest saying about time -- that what will be has already been -- which neither surrenders the corpus's presentism nor dismisses Ecclesiastes: what has already been is the form of futurity, the horizon-facing that every Now has always performed; what is nothing yet is the filling, the content no Now receives until it is the Now. The form is grounded; the filling is open. To Eternal Presence, which stands outside the figure, there is no not-yet at all.

## I. What Has Already Been Settled

This essay begins where another finished, and it is worth being exact about the boundary, because almost everything a reader wants to say about the future has already been said in this corpus and does not need saying again. [*When Is the Present?*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/ontology/philosophical%20theology/catholic%20philosophy/thomism/2026/07/03/when-is-the-present.html), in the section on the future, established the whole of the following. The future is not what lies ahead of the Now, because nothing lies ahead of a point that has no sides; it is the horizon of receptions not yet actualized. It is not a place and not a store of facts-in-waiting, and it is not nothing either; it is real in the mode proper to it, as horizon -- not predetermined, not unreal, uncondensed. The right loop of the lemniscate is not the future but anticipation, the interior's act of projecting over the horizon, fallible exactly as memory is fallible and for the same reason: it is an act, and acts can be wrong about what they reach for. And what is actualized at the crossing is decided by three co-determinants meeting there -- the traces, which are structural inertia, the settled topology along which the next becoming tends to run; the world's givens, the conditions that arrive uninvited, chosen by no one and received by all; and the response at the crossing, the stance of the will, the one place where something genuinely new enters reality. The future is therefore neither fate nor chance. Fate would be inheritance alone, the horizon collapsed into a corollary of the traces. Chance would be variation without gift. The horizon stands open between them, and reception's highest case remains grace, the arrival of what could never have come from what has been alone.

None of that is reopened here. It is the floor this essay stands on. The question that floor leaves standing is not what decides the future -- that is answered -- but what a horizon is, structurally, such that it can be faced and never entered. [*When Is the Present?*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/ontology/philosophical%20theology/catholic%20philosophy/thomism/2026/07/03/when-is-the-present.html) named the future's status. It did not state the future's law. The past received such a law in a later essay: [*What Is the Past?*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/metaphysics/philosophy-of-memory/philosophical-theology/2026/08/17/what-is-the-past-en.html) found that the operator distinguishing a past confluence from a present one is closure, the exhaustion of reception at a crossing. The future has a matching operator, and it has not yet been named. This essay names it.

## II. The Horizon No One Enters

Begin with the plainest fact about the future, so plain that it is normally mistaken for a triviality rather than read as a law. No one has ever been in the future. This is not a report about human finitude, though it includes one; it is a structural observation that would hold of any being whatever that lives at a Now. Whatever you call future is, without exception, what you are not yet at. The instant you are at it, it is not future -- it is the present, the actuality constituted at this crossing, and what is now future is something further out. You do not arrive in the future. You arrive at a Now, and the horizon has receded.

Watch the motion carefully, because the essay turns on it. Tomorrow is future. When tomorrow comes, you do not find yourself standing inside tomorrow-as-future; you find yourself at a present, and tomorrow has become today, and the future is now the day after. One second from now is future. Live that second and it is not future; it has converted, without residue, into a Now, and the future is the second after that. This is not a defect of short spans that longer ones escape. A decade from now is future in exactly the way one second from now is future, and it will be reached in exactly the same way, which is to say it will never be reached as future at all: it will be reached, if it is reached, as a present, the ten-years-hence horizon having receded, all the way down, into a Now that faces its own horizon still further out. The structure of futurity is the same at every scale. One second and one decade differ in magnitude and not in kind, because the property that makes each of them future -- being faced and not occupied -- is identical in both and is never, at any magnitude, lost by arrival.

The right word for this has to be chosen with care, because three words present themselves and each catches part of it. The future recedes: it withdraws before the advancing present at precisely the rate of advance, so that the distance between the Now and the future is not a distance that closes. The future is unreachable: not because it is far, but because reaching is the wrong verb for it -- there is no act of arrival that terminates in the future as future, since the terminus of every such act is a present. And the future is asymptotic: the present approaches it endlessly and coincides with it never, the way a curve approaches its asymptote through every finite magnitude without the gap ever becoming zero. The asymptote is not a curve hidden in time; it is the structure of futurity itself. Recession names the motion, unreachability names the impossibility, asymptote names the shape of the whole. They are one law under three descriptions, and the law is this: the future is that which the present approaches without limit and reaches at no point, because to reach it would be to end it.

## III. Why the Asymptote Is Not a Loss

A reader who has followed the past essays will feel a worry here, and it is the right worry to feel, because it is the exact mirror of one already answered about the past. Of the past it was asked: if it is closed and behind nothing, is it therefore gone, diminished, less than real? The answer was no -- pastness is not a diminution of reality but the closure of reception; the past is not less real than the present, only no longer open. The symmetric worry about the future is this: if the future is never reached, forever receding, is it therefore unreal, a mirage the present chases and never touches? And the answer is symmetric. Unreachability is not unreality. The future is not less real than the present for being unenterable; it is real in a different mode, the mode of the not-yet, exactly as the past is real in the mode of the no-longer-open. The mistake in both cases is the same: it measures reality by presence-at-the-Now, as though only what is occupying the crossing could count as real, when the whole burden of this topology has been that the present carries the closed past within it as constitution and faces the open future before it as horizon, and is thin without both.

There is even a sense in which the asymptote is the future's dignity rather than its poverty. A future that could be entered as future -- stood in, occupied, inspected while still future -- would be a future already actualized, which is to say a region of the block, a territory of facts-in-waiting, the very thing [*When Is the Present?*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/ontology/philosophical%20theology/catholic%20philosophy/thomism/2026/07/03/when-is-the-present.html) ruled out when it showed that the block fails because the past and the future are not co-actual with the present. The unreachability of the future is not a wall keeping us from something real on the other side. It is the same fact, seen from the traveler's side, as the future's openness seen from the metaphysician's: the future cannot be entered because there is nothing laid out in it to enter, and there is nothing laid out in it to enter because it is genuinely open, genuinely uncondensed, genuinely awaiting the reception that the will's response at the crossing will help decide. The receding horizon and the free act are one structure. Foreclose the recession -- let the future be a place you could get to while it is still future -- and you have foreclosed the act; you have handed the adversary the dataset that [*The Archive and the Now*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/philosophical-theology/christian-philosophy/2026/08/11/the-archive-and-the-now-en.html) denied him, who can only ever lay a wager on what you have tended to do, because there is no completed future for him to read. The asymptote is what keeps the next act unwritten.

## IV. What Will Be Has Already Been

Now the hardest saying, and the one this essay exists to meet, because a topology of time that leaves it unmet has left its central verse to its enemies. Qoheleth says: what has been is what will be; that which is to be, already has been. Read flatly, this is the block universe in the mouth of Scripture -- the future already laid out, all events pre-existing, nothing new, the traveler moving through a landscape whose far country is fixed before he arrives. Read that way, it contradicts everything this corpus has defended: it contradicts the openness of the horizon, the freedom of the act, the reality of grace as the arrival of what could not have come from what has been. So either the verse is wrong, or the corpus is, or the flat reading is. This essay's claim is that the flat reading is, and that the topology can say, more precisely than the flat reading ever could, what Qoheleth can be understood to have seen within this reading.

The instrument is the one [*What Is the Past?*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/metaphysics/philosophy-of-memory/philosophical-theology/2026/08/17/what-is-the-past-en.html) used on the word past, which turned out to be doing three jobs at once and dissolved its paradoxes the moment the jobs were separated. The word future is doing two, and they must be separated the same way. There is the future's form -- the horizon-facing itself, the standing structure by which a Now, any Now, is open toward a reception not yet actualized. And there is the future's filling -- the particular content that any given crossing in fact receives, this reception rather than that one, this frost, this word, this grace. These are not two futures. They are the form and the filling of one futurity, and everything turns on seeing that Qoheleth's saying is true of the first and false of the second.

Of the filling, nothing has already been. The content each Now receives is nothing yet until it is received; that is the whole meaning of the horizon's openness, and Qoheleth read against it would simply be mistaken. But of the form, what he says is exactly and eternally true. Every Now that has ever been actual faced a horizon precisely as this one does -- stood at a crossing, open toward what it had not yet received, about to convert a stretch of future into a present it would then carry as past. That structure has already been, without a single exception, in every present that has ever been constituted; it will be, without exception, in every present still to be constituted; there is nothing new under the sun in the one respect that the sun's risings are all, formally, the same rising, each a Now facing its own not-yet. What recurs is the crossing, not the cargo. Qoheleth saw the invariance of the form of temporal existence -- saw that to be a creature in time is always and everywhere to stand at this same open crossing -- and, within the topology, his saying can be understood as naming the invariance of temporal form in the only register the ancient text had available: that what will be has already been. Read this way, the saying is true of the form. The flat reading mistakes his true report about the form for a false report about the filling, and hears fatalism where he stated invariance.

This is why the same book that says nothing is new can say, three verses on, that God seeks what has been driven away. If the saying meant that every event pre-exists in a fixed future, that closing clause would be idle -- nothing driven away, nothing to seek, all of it already in place. The clause is not idle. It is at least compatible with a reading in which the filling remains genuinely open and genuinely sought, open to a recovery that the form alone does not supply; the recurrence of the form is precisely what leaves room for a God who seeks, rather than a mechanism that merely repeats. Ecclesiastes states the invariance and then, in the same breath, refuses the fatalism the invariance might seem to imply -- which is exactly the double motion this essay has been making, and exactly the reason the verse belongs at its head rather than in the hands of the block.

## V. The Future Grounded

One thing remains to be said, and it is the thing the intuition behind this essay reached for first, before any of the machinery was in place: that the future is grounded -- grounded as the present is, as the past was, as the Now is. The sentence is true and must be handled with care, because grounded is among the most load-bearing words this corpus owns, and the wrong reading of it would undo in one line everything the asymptote was built to protect. The future is not grounded as content awaiting arrival; there is no furnished future underwritten by God, no divine warehouse of things-to-come, for that would be the block again, wearing the robe of piety. What is grounded is the form, not the filling -- the Ground guarantees that futurity is open; it does not dictate what the openness receives -- and it is grounded because the form of the future is nothing other than the invariance of the crossing, the sameness of the one Now at which every reception is received, and that invariance is precisely what [*Does Time need Me, or do I need Time?*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/ontology/2026/04/10/does-time-need-me-or-do-i-need-time.html) located in the self-sustaining ground, the I AM WHO I AM who holds the single Now open. The horizon-facing has always been because the crossing has always been the same crossing, and the crossing has always been the same crossing because one ground, outside the figure, sustains it. The future's openness is grounded in the Now, and the Now in the Ground.

From the traveler's side, then, the future is asymptotic -- approached without limit, reached at no point, open at every scale. From the side of Eternal Presence, there is no asymptote and no not-yet at all, because Presence is not inside the figure to approach anything. [*When Is the Present?*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/ontology/philosophical%20theology/catholic%20philosophy/thomism/2026/07/03/when-is-the-present.html) already drew this line at its sharpest: God is not the largest of the presents, a creature's actuality infinitely extended; God is Eternal Presence, and Augustine said it to Him -- your today is eternity. Boethius named the mode: the whole, simultaneous, and perfect possession of unbounded life, the tota simul. What is horizon to the creature is not horizon to Presence; what is not yet to the one who stands at the crossing is not not-yet to the One who sustains the crossing, since the whole of what will be received is held, entire and at once, in the today that is eternity. To be eternally known is not to be temporally predetermined; eternal possession is not temporal pre-actualization, and the objection that this merely relocates the block into the mind of God fails exactly here. This is not the block returning by a back door. The block asserts that future events are co-actual with present ones on a single timeline, all equally there for a traveler within time. Presence asserts something the block cannot: that there is no timeline, that the creature genuinely stands at an open crossing with a genuinely unreached horizon, and that this whole open structure is possessed, all at once, by a Being who does not stand within it. The creature's not-yet is real. Presence's tota simul is real. They do not compete, because they are not the same standpoint, and only one of them is inside time.

<br>

## VI. Coda

The future, then, is the horizon the present faces and never enters -- not a place ahead, not a store of facts-in-waiting, not a diminished or a fated thing, but the open not-yet that recedes at the rate of every advance, the same at one second and at ten thousand years because it is faced at every scale and occupied at none. What has already been of it is only its form, the standing openness every Now has always worn. What is nothing yet is its filling, and will be nothing yet until the crossing that receives it is the Now. And the whole of it -- the receding horizon and the openness in which its filling can arise -- is held open by the one ground that holds the Now open, which guarantees the openness without dictating what it receives, and possessed entire, without succession and without not-yet, by the Eternal Presence in whose today the future has, in the only sense the block wanted and could never earn, already been.

The past is nothing lost. The future is nothing yet. And between them stands the present, alone actual, carrying the one and facing the other, at the crossing that has no width and is never, at any scale, left behind.
