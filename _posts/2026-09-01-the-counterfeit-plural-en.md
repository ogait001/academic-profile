## Note on Relation to Previous Works

This essay presupposes one result and cashes one debt. The result is the fourfold completed in [*Occupied Plurality: On the Family, the Community, and the We That Does Not Multiply the I*](https://oscargaitan.org/ontology/metaphysics-of-relations/theological-anthropology/2026/09/01/occupied-plurality-en.html): the four forms a created plural can take, sorted by whether the crossing points involved are occupied and whether what stands between them is dense. Legion is one point vacated and worn as many. Family and community are many points, each occupied, standing in a dense between borne at cost. The crowd is many points still occupied but standing in no dense between at all. And the algorithmic aggregate is the case at the far end from Legion — many real persons never gathered into occupancy of a shared between, represented instead as a we assembled from correlated traces.

<br>

That fourth case *Occupied Plurality* named and declined to develop, in a single sentence that identified it as a limit case "first identified in the draft work that led to this essay but not yet placed within a completed structure." It was placed on the grid and then set down. This essay takes it up. What follows is not a recapitulation of the fourfold but the development of its unfinished corner — and, with it, an answer to the question readers pressed after that essay appeared: what, exactly, distinguishes a genuine plural from a digitally assembled one, once we no longer permit ourselves the lazy answer that online communities simply are not real?

<br>

The method here is deliberate about its instruments and their offices, following the practice of [*De-Roling God: On Community, Multitude, and the Displacement of the Self from the Now*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/catholic-theology/ontology/2026/05/09/de-roling-god.html). Fuenteovejuna is a literary witness and a measuring instrument, not the proof of a metaphysical proposition. Legion is a theological and topological symbol, not a sociological verdict on any particular online group. The critique of the algorithmic aggregate is architectural and ontological — a claim about a structure of assembly, not a complaint about technology, and not a nostalgia for any prior arrangement of human life. The argument is meant to apply with equal force to a commercial fandom, a political movement, a religious following, and an outrage. The target is the form of aggregation, never the content aggregated.

<br>

## I. The Debt Occupied Plurality Left

A concept is not developed by being named. *Occupied Plurality* built a grid with four cells and filled three of them with argument — Legion at length, family and community as the essay's clean center, the crowd through Andersen's court. The fourth cell it filled with a description and a promise. The description was exact: a we "built from correlated traces — traces of the kind [*The Monad That Receives: On Leibniz, Indivisibility, and the Possibility of Relation*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/philosophy%20of%20mind/philosophy%20of%20religion/leibniz/2026/07/17/the-monad-that-receives.html) showed a single monad can rightly take up into its own act, but here taken up by no one, condensed into no single crossing point, and returned to the many as if it had been." The promise was that this cell would not be worked out there.

<br>

The promise was correct as editorial economy and costly as philosophy, because the fourth cell is the one the present moment actually inhabits. Few readers of that essay live inside Legion, and none live inside a seventeenth-century village. But almost all of them will, within the hour, be represented to themselves as a member of some we they never gathered to form — a following, a demographic, a cluster, an audience, a side. The essay diagnosed the condition of the age in a sentence and moved on. This essay stays in the sentence. The earlier essay deferred the fourth cell not from conceptual hesitation but from structural necessity: its load could not be carried without a dedicated treatment. The present essay is that treatment.

<br>

To stay in it requires the instrument the earlier essays kept reaching for without ever quite turning to this use. In [*Das Man and the First Person Singular*](https://oscargaitan.org/ontology/phenomenology/2026/04/21/das-man-and-the-first-person-singular.html), Fuenteovejuna was the counter-image to the anonymous court. In *De-Roling God*, it was the visible form of community against multitude. In *Occupied Plurality*, it was the first articulation of relational density, tested at its weakest member. Three times the village has been summoned as an example of what a real we looks like. It has not yet been used as what it most precisely is: a test. An example shows; a test measures. The difference is the whole of this essay.

<br>

## II. The Instrument, Not the Subject

Set the play down as an instrument and read only the part that measures. The killing of the Commander is not that part. The part that measures comes after, when the crown's investigators arrive and do the one thing that distinguishes a measuring procedure from a spectacle: they isolate. They take the villagers one at a time, apart from the others, and apply to each, alone, a cost severe enough that any ordinary bond would part under it. The interrogation is built on a single assumption, and the assumption is a hypothesis about relational structure: that when the cost is made individual, the collective will resolve into the individuals who were only ever its parts.

<br>

The hypothesis is falsified. "Fuenteovejuna lo hizo," each isolated villager answers, and the answer does not change under torture. The philosophically load-bearing fact is not the silence and not the courage. It is that the individual is placed under pressure without the individual disappearing. No one dissolves into the collective to escape the question; each remains a person, retaining an entire I, and from within that intact I assumes the common answer. This is the distinction *Occupied Plurality* named and this essay will lean its full weight upon: collective identity through erasure, where the person vanishes into the group, versus collective identity through integration, where the person remains and nonetheless bears the common burden. The village is the second. Its we is not a super-person composed of disposable members, nor a statistical sum of independent ones. It is integration without composition — a plural whose members stay persons while becoming able to carry something together. Composition would have made the we a super-entity built from parts; integration keeps the persons intact while relating them without remainder.

<br>

The corpus has established this reading twice already, and it will not be re-established here. What has not been done is to notice that the isolation scene is not a portrait of solidarity but an experiment upon it, complete with an apparatus, a controlled variable, and a result. The apparatus is the isolation. The variable is cost, applied to the member least able to bear it. The result is that the we holds. Once this is seen as an experiment rather than a tableau, it can be run again — on any candidate plural whatever, including the ones that did not exist when the play was written.

<br>

## III. What the Weakest Member Measures

Name precisely what the experiment measures. Relational density is the capacity of a plural to remain a plural when a serious cost falls upon its members individually. The definition is unusually strong because it is falsifiable: it does not ask how many say we, nor how loudly, nor how fervently they agree. It asks a single operational question with a determinate answer — what happens when one member has something real to lose.

<br>

The choice of member is not incidental; it is the essence of the measurement. The investigators do not test the village at its bravest. A structure tested at its strongest point tells you only about that point. This is the category mistake that confuses leadership with density: the cohesion produced by one exceptional member is not a property of the between but of the leader, and dissolves the moment he is removed. A chain is not rated by its strongest link, and a plural that holds only because one heroic member carries the rest has not demonstrated density — it has demonstrated leadership, which is a different property and a more fragile one, since it fails the moment that member is removed. Density is a property of the between, distributed across the members, and it is therefore measured where the between is thinnest: at the weakest occupant, under the heaviest individual cost. Fuenteovejuna passes there. Andersen's *The Emperor's* court, by contrast, fails instantly, because to alter the court's report it is enough to isolate any single courtier and change the social price of honest speech; nothing was ever foreclosed to hold the court's we together, so nothing has to be broken to dissolve it.

<br>

This yields the criterion in its provisional form. The density of a plural is measured not by the intensity of its agreement but by the cost its members can bear without abandoning one another. It is a philosophical criterion, not a laboratory measurement, and it should not be dressed as the latter. But it is provisional because, stated only this far, it proves too much — and the objection that shows why cannot be postponed.

<br>

## IV. What Density Does Not Prove

Cost alone will not carry the weight the argument has so far placed on it. The difficulty is not that the criterion is too weak but that, left here, it is indiscriminate. Pathologically dense plurals — the cult, the fused mob, the criminal fraternity — also bear cost without flinching; a criminal fraternity can be extraordinarily dense, holding its silence under exactly the pressure that breaks looser bonds; a totalitarian movement can demand, and receive, everything. Run the isolation experiment on any of these and it may well pass. If borne cost were sufficient for genuine community, the concept would extend to precisely the collectives it was meant to exclude, and Fuenteovejuna would prove that whatever a plural does together is thereby vindicated — which it plainly does not, since the village's answer is also, read without charity, an effective concealment of a killing.

<br>

The scene's ambiguity is worth admitting rather than smoothing away, because admitting it makes the reading stronger, not weaker. "Fuenteovejuna lo hizo" does not prove that the killing was just. It proves something narrower and more exact: that a plural has become capable of bearing consequence together, at its weakest member, without dissolving into the individuals who compose it. That is all the density criterion was ever entitled to establish. Density is a structural property, not a moral one. It reports that a we exists; it is silent on whether that we ought to exist in the form it has taken. A family can be dense and abusive. A relation can be real, weighted, mutually borne — and deforming. The instrument measures the reality of the bond, never its goodness.

<br>

So the criterion needs a second axis, and the corpus already supplies it, for this is the community-and-multitude distinction of *De-Roling God* raised one level. Genuine community is occupied crossing points standing in a dense between that does not require the annihilation of the person. Its pathological double — the cult, the mob at its most fused, the movement that consumes its members — has occupied points and a dense between as well, but binds them by subordinating the person to the collective rather than relating persons who remain intact. The difference is the one *Das Man* drew between a we that presupposes an I and a we produced by the subtraction of the I. Both are dense. Only one preserves what it binds. Relational density, then, is not merely shared cost. It is shared cost borne by irreducible persons for the sake of one another — the structure the corpus writes as I, then cost, then We, and never individual, then sacrifice, then collective, which is the grammar of fanaticism wearing community's face. Cost reveals whether a relational we exists at all. Preservation of the person tells us what kind of we it is. The legitimacy of a given dense we is a further question, downstream of both, and not one this essay needs to settle; it is enough here to have separated the two axes that the single word "community" had been carrying at once.

<br>

## V. Representation Without Occupancy

Now turn the instrument on the fourth case, and be exact about what is and is not being claimed, because the imprecise version of this argument is both common and easily destroyed.

<br>

The imprecise version says that relationships mediated by screens are not real. This is false and does not need to be defended, because it is not the claim. Persons meet through infrastructure, form genuine bonds, bear real costs for one another, and become, over time, a community with as much density as any village — and some do so having never met except through a network. Someone will say: I have friends online for whom I would suffer without hesitation. Just so — and the concession does not weaken the argument but states it. The friendship is dense because there has been actual relation between persons: memory, reciprocity, trust, vulnerability, cost borne and cost witnessed. The platform did not make that density. The persons did. State it as the proposition the rest of the section depends on: a medium can carry relational density; it cannot manufacture relational density by aggregating traces.

<br>

When density is present in a digitally mediated bond, it comes from the persons and the weighted crossings they have borne in one another's regard, never from the platform's correlation of their signals. The infrastructure hosted the relation; it did not constitute it. A genuine community that happens to be digitally mediated is a community first and digitally mediated second, and its density would survive the loss of any particular channel through which it presently runs.

<br>

The object of critique is the opposite construction, and it deserves its own name, which *Occupied Plurality* supplied in passing: representation without occupancy. The algorithmic aggregate is many real persons — the persons are not fictional, their emotions are not fabricated, their grievances may be entirely just — who have never come to occupy a shared between at all, and who are nonetheless represented to themselves as a we. The material of the representation is the trace: the like, the share, the follow, the purchase, the dwell-time, the outrage. A trace, in the corpus's grammar, is the minimal unit of behavior a single monad can rightly condense into its own act — memory without relation, signal without reciprocity. *The Monad That Receives* established that a trace is exactly the kind of thing a single crossing point can rightly take up and condense into its own act. The aggregate performs the outward form of that operation with the inward reality removed. The traces are gathered, correlated, and returned to the many as a we — but they were condensed into no crossing point, taken up by no occupant, integrated by no one. It is, in the phrase the earlier essay reached for and then set down, a between with nothing between it: not merely thin, the way the crowd's between is thin, but structurally incapable of density in the first place, because density accrues to weighted crossings borne by occupants, and here there is no occupancy for any crossing to be weighted against.

<br>

There is a stranger feature still, and it is the sharpest edge of the concept. In a genuine community the representation follows the relation — the chronicle is written after the village has lived. In the aggregate the order reverses: the representation precedes the relation it claims to represent. The system tells me that people like me think this, buy this, hate this, belong here, are us — and it tells me before I have encountered a single one of the persons the us is said to contain. The belonging arrives ahead of anyone to belong with. This is the same inversion [*Non te egeo - I do not need you: When We Stopped Asking*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/ontology/2026/05/16/non-te-egeo.html) found in the life of the question, now working on the life of the we: there the answer was supplied before the hunger that would have sought it; here the membership is supplied before the gathering that would have formed it.

<br>

Two consequences follow, and each is a clean distinction the softer vocabulary of "online community" cannot draw. The first is that the aggregate delivers a population, not a set of persons. It can say with precision that eighty-four percent of people like me believe some thing — but who are the eighty-four percent, and what stands between any two of them? A census counts; it does not relate. It can tell you how many exist and tell you nothing about whether they constitute anything, because counting persons and relating persons are different operations and only the second builds a between. The second consequence is the absence of reciprocity, which is what a between runs on. In the village the individual bears the cost because the others matter to him and his standing matters to them; the relation runs in both directions, and that mutual, weighted regard is the between itself. The aggregate guarantees none of it. I can be represented as a member of a demographic we without any other member knowing me, depending on me, owing me anything, or being willing to bear the smallest cost for me. And so the aggregate's deepest poverty can be stated in one line: it can contain millions of people without establishing that any of them belong to one another.

<br>

This is why the aggregate is the far pole from Legion rather than a variant of the crowd. Legion is one crossing point vacated and then worn as many — a subtraction of the singular. The crowd is many points still occupied but joined by nothing dense — a relation too cheap to hold. The aggregate is neither a vacated point nor a thin relation but the form of relation produced where no relation was formed: the appearance of a between manufactured out of behavior that never crossed between anyone. Visibility, here, is not evidence of density. It is the substitute manufactured in its place.

<br>

## VI. When the Cost Arrives

The instrument now has a use it was built for. Ask of any apparent we the question the investigators asked the village: what happens when the cost arrives? The aggregate answers the question by its architecture before any particular case is examined, because everything the aggregate is built to reward is inexpensive. One click. One repost. One comment. One changed profile picture. One expression of outrage costing nothing and foreclosing nothing. Participation at this price can accumulate to enormous visible size — and visible size is precisely what the architecture is optimized to produce and display.

<br>

But the play's test is not a test of size. It is a test of what remains when participation stops being cheap: when it requires physical presence, sustained sacrifice, the protection of another person at cost to oneself, exposure to real risk, long-term responsibility, material cooperation, and the far less photogenic labor of rebuilding after the attention has moved on. This is the seam along which mobilization and community come apart. An aggregate mobilizes with a speed no village could match; a village bears a cost no aggregate has shown it can bear. The two capacities are not the same capacity, and the metrics that measure the first are structurally silent about the second.

<br>

Three compressions state the seam. Virality measures transmission; it does not measure solidarity. Coordination measures synchronization; it does not measure community. Visibility measures representation; it does not measure presence. Each names a quantity the aggregate can produce in abundance and a quantity it cannot produce at all, and each pair looks identical from outside and proves opposite the instant a real cost falls on a real member. And here the asymmetry that most alarms observers finds its structural explanation. A low-density aggregate is formidable at destruction and feeble at construction, because destruction can be transacted in cheap participation — the boycott, the pile-on, the delegitimation, the shaming — while construction demands exactly the dense between the aggregate never formed: persistence, institutions, trust, the distribution of cost, the protection of weaker members, and cooperation that outlasts the news cycle. Destruction is cheap because it requires no reciprocity and no preservation of the person; construction demands both. A crowd can be assembled faster than a community can be built, and torn down faster than either. Density is the thing that must be present for building and can be dispensed with entirely for burning.

<br>

## VII. The We That Can Be Edited

There is a second vulnerability, deeper than cost and independent of it, and it belongs to the aggregate's mode of existence rather than to any test applied from outside. A dense community exists independently of its representation. The village would be the same village if every chronicle of it were burned; its we is carried in the persons and their borne crossings, which no external hand administers. The aggregate has no such independence, because its members encounter one another only through infrastructure none of them controls, and its we exists only in the continuous act of being represented.

<br>

This is the argument *Occupied Plurality* gestured toward and this essay can now state as a proposition: a plural whose existence depends entirely upon the representation of its relations is at the disposal of whoever controls the representation. The apparent we can be altered — enlarged, shrunk, inflamed, dispersed — by adjusting ranking, recommendation, visibility, moderation, search, amplification, suppression, or the design of the interface, without a single member changing their mind about anything. This is not first of all a claim about censorship, though censorship is one instrument of it. It is an architectural claim: the platform does not merely host the plural. It participates, continuously and constitutively, in determining what the plural appears to be. A community discovers what it is by living; an aggregate is told what it is by the system that renders it, and can be told something else tomorrow.

<br>

## VIII. The Manufacture of the Plural

The preceding section established that the aggregate's belonging is supplied before the gathering, on the pattern *Non te egeo* found in the life of the question. Editability is the passive form of the aggregate's dependence; manufacturability is its active form. What can be altered can also be assembled. One consequence of that inversion was left unstated there and must be drawn now, because it accounts for the feature that most alarms observers: why a low-density aggregate is not merely fragile under cost but unusually easy to assemble, to synchronize, to inflame, to point.

<br>

A member whose belonging was supplied rather than formed has done none of the weighted crossing that would make the we dense. He was never asked to gather with anyone; he was represented as already gathered, and received the representation the way he received every other pre-navigated conclusion — as convenience, without noticing that anything was skipped. That absence is not only what makes the aggregate feeble when a real cost arrives. It is, at the same time and for the same reason, what makes it frictionless to move. The weighted crossings that would have made the we able to bear a cost are the very crossings that would have given it internal resistance to being re-pointed; lacking them, it can be synchronized at a speed no dense community could match, precisely because there is nothing borne to hold it in place.

<br>

This is why the manufacture of consensus is a structural possibility and not merely an abuse. A fringe position can be made to appear dominant; automated participation can imitate the signs of real participation; escalation can be rewarded until it looks like conviction — not because the persons are fictional or their feeling false, but because an aggregate assembled from cheap signals has no internal resistance to being re-assembled from cheaper ones. The non serviam was a wound in a relation; the non te egeo was the painless closure of the space where the relation might have formed. The counterfeit plural is that closure at the scale of the we: the appearance of a gathering produced without the gathering, offered to persons too supplied with belonging to notice they never came together.

<br>

## IX. Two Grammars of the Many

Set the two decisive utterances beside one another, as *Occupied Plurality* set Legion beside the family, and hear that they are opposite where they sound alike.

<br>

Fuenteovejuna says: we did it. Legion says: we are many. Both are first-person plurals; they are structurally inverse. Fuenteovejuna is many intact I's resolving into one responsible We — a plural spoken from a first person singular that survives in each member, at cost. Legion is one I collapsed into an undifferentiated many — a plural spoken where no first person remains to speak it, multiplicity worn as a wound. Its multiplicity is not plurality but injury — the subtraction of the singular that makes relation impossible. The village does not destroy the I in order to make the We; it requires the I in order to make the We. Genuine plurality is not the multiplication of selves. It is the relation of intact selves.

<br>

The algorithmic aggregate is then locatable with precision: it is the technological environment in which Legion's condition can be reproduced at scale and without possession. *De-Roling God* argued that the operation which finally succeeds against the ground does not refute it but evacuates the self from the crossing point where the ground could be felt. The aggregate is the collective face of that same evacuation. It does not gather intact I's into a responsible We, as the village does; it does not even, as Legion does, hold one contested point open. It produces the grammar of the many — the visible, sayable, shareable we are many — over persons who were never gathered into a we at all, and so extends across a population the exact condition Legion suffered in one body: plurality where no singular is present to ground it. Fuenteovejuna's we is dense because its persons were gathered. The aggregate's we is a representation because its persons were only ever counted.

<br>

## X. The Investigators Arrive

Return the instrument to the scene that calibrated it, and imagine the crown's investigators arriving not in a village but in a genuinely digital Fuenteovejuna — a we of a million, visible, synchronized, fluent in one voice. The investigators do what they always do. They isolate. They raise the cost. They ask each member, alone and exposed, to bear what the village bore.

<br>

The point is not that no one would remain. Some would; some genuine communities are digitally mediated, and some persons are dense with others they met through a network and would answer as the village answered. The point is narrower and harder to evade: the metrics cannot tell you which. A million likes cannot answer the investigators' question. A million followers cannot. A million reposts cannot. The number that the architecture produces and displays — the very quantity offered as proof of the we — is precisely the quantity that goes silent when the cost arrives, because it measured transmission and synchronization and visibility, and the investigators are asking about presence and solidarity and borne cost. Only persons standing at real relational cost can answer, and whether the aggregate contains them is exactly what its representation cannot show.

<br>

So Fuenteovejuna is not, in the end, an argument against digital communication, and not a lament for a world before the network. It is an argument against a single confusion — the confusion of aggregation with community — and it is a decisive one, because it supplies the question that confusion cannot survive. Not how many are we, which the aggregate answers superbly and irrelevantly. But what can we bear together without ceasing to be the persons who compose us. A crowd can agree without knowing one another. A network can synchronize without becoming a community. An algorithm can represent a we without creating one. But a genuine We becomes visible only when its members can bear the weight of one another without ceasing to be I — and that is the one thing no quantity of signals will ever show, and the one thing the isolation scene was built to reveal. The counterfeit plural has everything the genuine one has, except the only thing that was ever the point.
