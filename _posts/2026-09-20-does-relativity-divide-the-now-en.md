## Note on Sources and Method

This essay is a companion to the temporal sequence and to [*What Sustains the Now?*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/philosophical-theology/philosophy-of-god/2026/09/18/what-sustains-the-now-en.html) and [*Does Time need Me, or do I need Time?*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/ontology/2026/04/10/does-time-need-me-or-do-i-need-time.html), whose topology it recalls rather than re-derives: the lemniscate whose crossing is the Now, whose loops are the present acts of memory and anticipation, and whose ground is the Non-Derivative that is no part of the figure. It presupposes, too, the corpus's discipline about physics. The results it uses are established ones — the relativity of distant simultaneity, time dilation, and proper time as an invariant along a specified worldline — and nothing here asserts of physics what physics does not assert. In particular, the reading of these results in terms of actuality is philosophical inference, and is marked as such where it is made; physics establishes the relativity of simultaneity, not a doctrine of the Now.
This essay also does something the earlier ones did not: it corrects a form of expression in the foundational essay. Where *Does Time Need Me, or Do I Need Time?* spoke of events "actualized in the same Now, simultaneously" and of "one shared present," it borrowed the physicist's vocabulary of simultaneity and overspent it. The central doctrine it meant — that actuality is one condition — is retained; the implication that this oneness requires one shared simultaneity is withdrawn, and the wording is corrected accordingly. This is set down plainly, because a system matures more honestly by naming the sentence a theory exposed than by insisting the sentence was never loose. The witnesses invoked — Einstein and Minkowski, Boethius and Aquinas, and, on the problem of eternity, Stump and Kretzmann — are parallel witnesses and company on the road, not authorities from which the argument descends; it stands or falls on its own structural coherence. And the discipline of the corpus holds here as elsewhere: physics is not asked to establish metaphysical personal identity, the indivisibility of the soul, or the unreality of branching histories. Those are argued elsewhere, on other grounds, and are not smuggled in under a Lorentz scalar.

## Abstract

A traveler circles the earth ten times at nearly the speed of light and returns younger than the clock he left behind. The popular name for this is time travel, and the popular picture beneath the name is spatial: the future as a territory lying ahead, the traveler as one who covers ground to reach it. This essay uses the traveler as a door and not as a subject, for what he exposes is not a fact about corridors but a question about the Now — and, with it, the one sentence in the corpus that a physicist may legitimately break. Special relativity abolishes any frame-independent relation of simultaneity among spacelike-separated events; there is no unique decomposition of the world into one space existing at a common time. Read as a claim about distant events, the corpus's "one shared present" is exactly the cosmic present relativity denies. The essay answers not by defending the sentence but by unbundling the word beneath it. There are two Nows: the physicist's simultaneity-now, a frame-relative relation reaching across space, and the topology's actuality-Now, the condition under which a continuant is actual at an event on its own worldline. The decisive point is categorical and not metrical: the crossing is not a simultaneity surface at all, and so is not the kind of thing relativity divides. Proper time then supplies an invariant measure along the continuant's worldline — invariant, though path-dependent — which keeps succession real without turning it into passage through a territory. One Now is accordingly redefined: not the numerical unity of a cosmic slice, but the invariance of the form of actualization, one condition operative identically at every crossing. The theological consequence is drawn last and drawn carefully. Divine eternity is not the possession of a privileged frame in which all events are at last simultaneous; the totum simul belongs to the Ground and introduces no succession into it, so that temporally separated crossings are present to God without being simultaneous with one another. The crossings need not share a temporal coordinate in order to share a Ground. Einstein, in this reading, takes from the corpus only a sentence, and returns a purer one: he purges from the account of the Now its last residue of the territory.

## I. The Traveler's Question

Let a man be given the one thing the universe will not give him: a speed just short of light's, and a body that could bear it. He sets out to circle the earth, and he does it ten times, and he comes home. He has been gone, by every clock in the room he returns to, a little over a second. By the clock he carried, he has been gone a seventh of that. He is, in the plainest and most literal sense the word will bear, younger than he would have been had he stayed — younger than the room, younger than the friend who watched him go. And the word that leaps to the tongue for what he has done is travel: he has traveled into the future, arrived ahead of schedule, reached a time the rest of us have not yet come to.

Hold the word up to the light and watch what it carries. Travel is a spatial verb. To travel is to leave one place and arrive at another, to cross the ground between a here and a there that waited, furnished and real, before the traveler came and will remain after he goes. Buried in the phrase travel into the future is a picture the corpus has spent every essay refusing: the future as a territory, laid out ahead like a country to the north, into which the swift may press while the slow lag behind. On that picture the traveler's feat is locomotion — he has simply covered temporal distance faster than we, the way a fast car reaches the next city first — and the future he has reached was there all along, a place, waiting to be entered.

This essay takes the traveler as a door and not as its subject. What he opens onto is not a corridor but a question, and the question is about the Now. For if the whole corpus is right that the future is not a territory, and that the right loop is anticipation — the continuant's present act toward what may be — then the traveler cannot have entered a future country lying ahead of him. He can only have done what everyone does, at every crossing, always: be actual. The task is to say exactly how that is true when the clocks, at reunion, undeniably disagree — and to say it without flinching from the one place where relativity aims a legitimate blow at the corpus's own words.

<br>

## II. What Relativity Actually Says

Take the numbers first, as an idealized thought experiment and nothing more; the practical impossibilities are total and beside the point. The earth's circumference is near forty thousand kilometers; ten circuits are four hundred thousand. At ninety-nine hundredths of light's speed the journey takes, in the earth's reckoning, about one and a third seconds. But a clock moving that fast keeps time more slowly, by a factor here of about seven, so the traveler's own clock records only about a fifth of a second. When he sets his clock beside the one that stayed, the two readings differ, and the difference is not an illusion, an error, or a matter of opinion. Clocks do this. Atomic clocks flown around the world return disagreeing with the clocks that stayed; unstable particles moving near light-speed live measurably longer than those at rest; the satellites of the Global Positioning System must be corrected for exactly these effects, and for their gravitational companions, or the system's fixes would drift beyond use within a day. The slowing is as well established as anything in physics.

Be precise, though, about what slows and what does not. The quantity that differs between the two is proper time: the time elapsed along a particular worldline, the reading a clock carried along that path actually accumulates. Proper time is invariant — every frame agrees how much a given clock ticked between two events on its own line — but it is path-dependent: two worldlines that part at one event and meet again at another can accumulate different amounts of it, and generally do. That, and nothing stranger, is the whole content of the so-called twin paradox, which is not a paradox in the theory. The traveler and the room take different paths between the same two events, their parting and their reunion; the paths accumulate different amounts of proper time; the clocks read accordingly. There is no symmetry to trouble the result: between departure and reunion the traveler and the room follow different worldlines and accumulate different proper times. In the idealized experiment, the traveler must repeatedly change direction in order to return, so their histories are not related by a single inertial-frame symmetry.

Distinguish proper time, in turn, from coordinate time, which is a different thing: a temporal coordinate assigned within a chosen reference system, which may be physically realized by a grid of synchronized clocks but is not identical with the proper time accumulated by every clock within that system. The Positioning System builds such a coordinate deliberately, a shared temporal grid for the whole constellation. And here relativity says the thing that matters most for what follows. For spacelike-separated events, coordinate simultaneity is not unique. Different inertial frames disagree about which distant events happen "at the same time," and no frame's verdict is privileged over another's. There is no frame-independent way to gather the far-flung events of the world into one space existing at a common instant. The universal present, the cosmic now spread across all of space, is precisely what special relativity removes.

One datum survives every frame untouched, and it will carry the argument. When the traveler and the room are reunited, they are at one event — co-located, in one place at one moment of contact — and about that single event there is no disagreement. Frames dispute the simultaneity of distant events; they never dispute what a given clock reads at an event on its own line, and they never dispute that a single event is one event. A spacetime event is not multiplied or divided by a change of inertial frame; what changes is its coordinate description and its relation of simultaneity to other, spacelike-separated events. The whole relativity of simultaneity is a relativity among the separated. It says nothing against the actuality of a continuant where it actually is.

<br>

## III. The Objection at Full Strength

Now let the objection come, and come at full strength, for a softened form of it would not be worth the answer. The foundational essay of this corpus wrote, of the billions of lives underway at any moment:

*They are actualized in the same Now, simultaneously, each as a distinct event within the one shared present… Every being that exists, exists now.*

Set that sentence in front of a physicist and he has a clean and legitimate reply. "The same Now, simultaneously" and "one shared present" are, on their face, a claim about distant events — about the sleeper and the newborn and the traveler all belonging together to a single present that spans the space between them. And that is the one claim relativity forbids. There is no such present. There is no frame-independent slice of the world, no hypersurface of the simultaneous, that gathers spatially separated lives into a common now; every inertial frame determines its simultaneity relation differently, and none is uniquely privileged by special relativity. The corpus, in this sentence, did not merely say something the physicist finds unfamiliar. It borrowed his own word, simultaneous, and asserted with it precisely the thing his best theory struck down a century ago.

The objection is sound, and the honest course is to grant it without qualification. The sentence is not defended in what follows. It is corrected. But the correction, rightly made, does not cost the corpus its Now; it costs the corpus a word it should never have borrowed — and in returning the word it recovers the Now in a purer form.

<br>

## IV. The Two Nows

The whole confusion, as so often in this topology, lives in a single word doing two jobs. Past was found doing three; future, two; stops, in the essay on the physical order, two again. Here the word is now, and behind it present and simultaneous, and the two jobs it does are so unlike that a claim true in the one is false in the other.

There is, first, the simultaneity-now: a relation among spacelike-separated events by which a chosen inertial frame assigns them the same time coordinate. This is the physicist's now, and it is the now of Minkowski's slice — a spacelike surface cutting through the whole of space at what one frame calls a single time. It is a claim about the far: about how this event here stands, in respect of time, to that event there. And it is exactly this now that relativity relativizes, because the surface is not unique and the coexistence it asserts is a matter of the frame that asserts it.

There is, second, the actuality-now: the condition under which a continuant is actual at an event on its own worldline. This is the Now of the topology, the crossing of the figure. It is not a claim about the far at all. It says nothing of how this life stands, in time, to a life across the world; it says that this continuant, here, at this event of its own line, is actual — has crossed from the potential of anticipation and the fixity of memory into the one mode that is ever real. It reaches across no space. It gathers no distant events. It is the actuality of one, where the one is.

The foundational essay meant the second and wrote the first. It meant that every being is actual only in the mode of the crossing — that there is no other mode in which anything is ever real — and it reached, to say so, for the word simultaneously, which says instead that all those beings share one slice of coordinate time. The two are not the same claim, and only the first was ever the doctrine. Which yields the essay's marked inference, and it is marked because the word actuality is the topology's and not physics': special relativity does not teach that there is no actuality; it teaches that actuality cannot be represented as one frame-independent simultaneity surface extending across space. The first half is interpretation; the second is the theorem. Relativity refutes the surface. It never reached the crossing, because the crossing was never the surface.

<br>

## V. The Slice and the Crossing

It is tempting to say the crossing survives relativity because it has no width — that a slice can be divided where a point cannot. The temptation must be refused, because the reason is wrong and a wrong reason is a breach in the wall. A relativistic surface of simultaneity is itself of zero temporal thickness; it is a slice in space, not in time, thin as an instant. Thinness is not what distinguishes the crossing from it. If the crossing's safety rested on having no width, the slice could claim the same safety, and the wall would fall.

The distinction is not metrical but categorical. A simultaneity surface is a hypersurface: a thing extended through space that reaches out to distant events and assigns them to a common coordinate-time slice, and whose defect under relativity is that it is not unique — each frame draws its own, and none is the world's own. The crossing is not a hypersurface. It reaches out to nothing. It binds no distant events. It makes no assertion whatever about what coexists elsewhere, and so there is nothing in it for a change of frame to relativize. It is the actuality of a single continuant at a single event of its worldline, and that is a category the relativity of simultaneity does not touch — not because relativity fails to divide it, but because it is not the kind of thing relativity divides.

So the decisive line is not that one cannot slice what has no width. It is this: relativity cannot divide the Now of the topology, because that Now is not a simultaneity surface joining distant events but the condition of actuality at the crossing. The same condition is operative at every crossing of every worldline; that sameness is not a shared coordinate time. And from this the proposition falls out that the mature topology is built to hold: every continuant is actual only at its crossing; it does not follow that the crossings of distinct continuants are simultaneous with one another. Sameness of condition is not sameness of coordinate. The Now is one as a condition is one — not as a slice is one.

<br>

## VI. The Measure Along the Continuant

Return now to the traveler, with the word repaired. Physics offers, along his worldline, an invariant it does not offer across worldlines: the proper time elapsed along his particular path, on which every frame agrees, and which differs from the room's not by convention but because the paths themselves differ. This is worth holding onto, because it tells us what relativity took and what it left. It took the global slice — the one present laid across all of space — and left, untouched, an invariant measure along each individual line. There is no world-wide now on which frames agree; there is, for each continuant, a real and frame-independent quantity of elapsed time along its own history, path-dependent and intrinsic to that worldline.

But keep the categories clean, or the gain is lost. Proper time is not the crossing. The crossing is the actuality of the continuant; proper time is the measure along the sequence of events through which that actuality passes. Nor is coordinate time the crossing; it is the frame's bookkeeping. Nor are the loops coordinate time; memory and anticipation are the continuant's present acts, its reaching toward what has been and what may be, and they are no more a coordinate assignment than grief is a grid reference. Four things, and only confusion joins them: coordinate time, the frame's convention; proper time, the invariant along the worldline; the loops, the present acts of memory and anticipation; and the crossing, the actuality itself — which is no clock reading, not coordinate time and not proper time, but the being-actual whose passage the measures measure.

With that held, the traveler's whole journey is transparent. At no event of his ten circuits is he anywhere but at his own crossing. His clock accumulates less than the room's because less proper time elapses along his path; but shortness of path is not entry into a corridor, and less elapsed proper time is not arrival in a territory. He does not pass from a present, down a road, into a future that awaited him. He is, at each event of his line, simply actual — and then again actual, and again: Now, and Now, and Now, where Now means successive actuality at events on his worldline and never a universal cosmic present he shares with the room he left. When he comes home younger, he has not been to the future. He has been, the whole time, exactly where every continuant always is: at the crossing, which he never left, because it is not a place one leaves.

One consequence may be drawn, provided it is drawn no further than it reaches. Relativity removes the invariant global slice but preserves an invariant temporal measure along the individual worldline; the history of a particular continuant is not dissolved into arbitrary coordinate convention. This makes the topology physically compatible with an invariant structure of the continuant's own succession — which is a real and welcome result. It is not more than that. A Lorentz scalar does not establish the indivisibility of the soul, and physics does not carry one from an invariant measure to a metaphysics of personal identity; the essays that argue for the one unrepeatable self argue on other grounds, and are not reinforced by conscripting relativity to a task it cannot perform. What may be said, and only this, is that relativity raises no obstacle here that the corpus must clear. It removes the global slice and leaves the worldline and its accumulated proper time as invariant structures, and the topology asks of it nothing more.

<br>

## VII. One Now Without One Simultaneity

We may now say exactly what the mature topology means, and no longer means, by the oneness of the Now. It does not mean that all actual beings occupy a single simultaneous slice of time; that was the borrowed sentence, and relativity was right to take it. It means that actuality has one invariant form. There is one condition — actual-only-at-the-crossing — and it is operative identically on every worldline, whatever that line's proper time, whatever its motion relative to any other. The Now is one as a condition is one, not as a coordinate is one; its unity is the unity of a form of actualization, not the numerical unity of a shared present.

This keeps everything the foundational essay wanted from the One Now except the single thing it should not have wanted. The plurality of events was always granted — sleeping and writing and grieving were always different acts. What is granted now, in addition, is the frame-relativity of distant simultaneity: that there is no fact of the matter, frame-independent, about which of those acts is simultaneous with which. And neither plurality — not the plurality of events, not the frame-relativity of their simultaneity — multiplies the condition. Under all of it, one form of actuality: each continuant real only at its crossing, none of them anywhere else, none of them ever in any mode but the present of its own line. A guard belongs here, lest the repair be taken for a surrender: that each continuant is actual at its crossing, and that no frame-independent fact makes distinct crossings simultaneous, is not the claim that every event is equally actual merely because each is actual at itself. That actuality is the crossing's alone remains a substantive proposition of the topology — conceded to no eternalism here, and demanding of relativity no proof of presentism in exchange; it is argued on its own ground in *What Is the Now?* and in the work on condensation, and the present essay shows only that relativity raises against it no objection it must clear.

The crossings of the sleeper and the newborn and the traveler need not share a coordinate time. Relativity has shown that no frame-independent fact selects one distant simultaneity relation as the world's own, and the topology no longer needs one. They share, instead, a condition — and, if the argument of the corpus holds, a Ground. It is to the second that the essay finally turns, because the abolition of the cosmic present, so far from embarrassing the theology, clears from it an idol it was always in danger of worshipping.

<br>

## VIII. Eternity Is Not Simultaneity

There is a way of saving God from relativity that must be refused, and refusing it is the whole of the theological gain. Once the universal present is gone from physics, the tempted believer reaches to restore it in God: to say that if no frame within the world is privileged, God at least occupies the true one — the frame from which all events turn out, after all, to be simultaneous, the vantage from which the cosmic slice is finally read correctly. This is to make God the master clock. It reinstates the abolished present under a holy name and, worse, it puts God inside the figure as its largest frame, one more observer, supremely placed. A god who is the privileged frame is a god within the geometry, and the corpus has said from the first that the Ground is no member of what it grounds.

Classical eternity was always more radical than a best vantage, and here is its hour. Boethius named eternity the whole, simultaneous, and perfect possession of unending life; Aquinas held that eternity is tota simul, all at once, precisely because there is no succession in God, and distinguished it even from the temporal now. The totum simul is the Ground's and not the universe's. It does not mean that God stands where all events are seen to happen together. It means that the succession of events introduces no succession into the Ground: that what is, for the creature, one crossing after another is, for God, not a sequence He undergoes but a whole He holds — not because He has found the frame in which the sequence flattens into a slice, but because He is not in the sequence at all.

The distinction has a known difficulty, and the corpus meets difficulties rather than stepping around them. If the burning of Rome is present to eternity, and the writing of this sentence is present to eternity, does it not follow, by the ordinary transitivity of "present together," that the burning of Rome is present to the writing of this sentence — that all of time collapses into one instant after all? Philosophers of divine eternity have felt the force of this and have had to distinguish the relation temporal things bear to eternity from the relation they bear to one another; Stump and Kretzmann built a whole account around just such a relation, so that events could be present to eternity without being present to each other. The topology has a native form of the same answer, and it is cleaner in its own idiom. The relation a crossing bears to the Ground is not the relation one crossing bears to another. The first is not a temporal relation at all, and transitivity across the two kinds is simply invalid. Rome's burning and this writing need not be simultaneous with each other in order to be present, each at its own crossing, to a single non-successive act. Which is the line the essay was built to reach: the crossings need not share a temporal coordinate in order to share a Ground.

So I AM WHO I AM is not the master clock. He is not the frame in which the world is at last simultaneous with itself. He is that to which every crossing is present without any crossing being, to Him, past or future — and without the crossings being, to one another, simultaneous. Relativity, having taken the cosmic present out of physics, has not taken anything the theology needed; it has removed the very idol the theology was forever being mistaken for. When the Gospel has the eternal speak across the widest reach of time — before Abraham was, I am — it does not assert that Abraham's age and the speaker's are one moment of coordinate time. It asserts a presence that owes nothing to simultaneity: the present tense of the one who does not say I was, held across a temporal distance it does not need to close, because it was never spread along the distance to begin with.

<br>

## IX. A Formal Statement

Three sentences, offered as the definitions this essay exists to fix.

The Now is one not because all actual beings occupy a single simultaneous slice of time, but because actuality has one invariant form: every temporal continuant is actual only at its crossing. Special relativity denies that the crossings of distinct continuants possess any frame-independent relation of simultaneity; it does not thereby turn any crossing into past or future for the continuant whose actuality it is. Proper time measures succession along the worldline — invariantly, though path-dependently — without converting that succession into passage through a temporal territory.

What relativity divides is the simultaneity surface, a hypersurface reaching across space to bind distant events into a coexisting present; what it cannot divide is the crossing, which is no hypersurface, reaches out to nothing, and asserts nothing of what coexists elsewhere. The Now is not the kind of thing relativity divides.

The divine totum simul is not the possession of a privileged frame in which all events are at last simultaneous, but the presence of every crossing to a Ground into which their succession introduces no succession: the crossings need not share a temporal coordinate in order to share a Ground.

<br>

## X. Coda

The traveler keeps everything. He keeps his ten circuits and his younger clock; he keeps the seventh of a second that is really all he lived while the room lived more; he keeps a history along his own line that is his and no one else's and could not have been traded for the room's. Nothing true of him is taken back. What is taken back is only a picture — the corridor he never walked, the territory he never entered, the future that was never a place lying in wait for the swift. He did not travel into it. He did what the sleeper did and the newborn did and the room did: he was actual, at his crossing, and then again, and could no more leave the Now than a thing can leave the condition of its being real — for to leave the crossing is not to arrive elsewhere but to cease.

And the corpus keeps everything too, having given up a word. It gave up simultaneously, and one shared present, and it was right to; those were the physicist's, and lent on terms the physicist was entitled to call. But the Now was never the loan. The oneness it sought was never the oneness of a when laid across the heavens, which relativity was right to deny; it was the oneness of a condition, which relativity never reached. And the holding of every crossing at once — of Rome and this sentence and the traveler's tenth circuit, each at its own present, none of them simultaneous, all of them held — was never a feat the universe could perform from some best seat within itself. It belongs to the One who is not in the figure, to whom the whole of it is present, and who answers the question of what holds the crossings not with the name of a frame but with a name that has no tense but the present, and no succession, and no outside:

**I AM WHO I AM**
