## Note on Sources and Method

This essay is a synthesis, and extends four prior essays of this corpus rather than re-deriving them: [*The Infinite Interior*](https://oscargaitan.org/ontology/philosophy-of-time/2026/05/05/the-infinite-interior.html), which found that a self persists through change not across a seam but along a continuous interior; [*Whose Condensation Am I?*](https://oscargaitan.org/phenomenology/christian-theology/philosophical-anthropology/2026/08/07/whose-condensation-am-i-en.html), which found that a life is a convergence no single ancestor completes; [*The Chicken, the Egg, and the Man Who Was Not Hatched*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy/metaphysics/philosophy%20of%20religion/2026/07/11/the-chicken-the-egg-and-the-man.html), which distinguished the becoming of a subject already underway from the origin of a subject that was not; and [*Is the Beginningless Grounded?*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/philosophy-of-time/divine-eternity/2026/09/25/is-the-beginningless-grounded-en.html), which distinguished having no first member of a series from being no member of the series at all. It keeps the discipline those essays kept. Its method is a single one: it takes one word — before — and finds that it is not one question but three, the third of which before cannot finally answer. Where the argument touches the origin of the soul it marks, as the corpus always marks, the line between what is defended as a philosophical proposition and what is received as a doctrinal reading, and claims for the proposition only the weaker and surer form. The witnesses named along the way — Augustine, Boethius, Aquinas — are parallel witnesses and company on the road, not authorities from which the argument descends; it stands or falls on its own structural coherence.

<br>

## Abstract

A meditation returns to the oldest circular question — which came first, the chicken or the egg — and notices that the question treats one thing as two. The egg is the chicken not yet, as the boy is the man not yet; to ask which was first is to ask what was before oneself. This essay follows that noticing until the chicken hands the question to the first person, where it was always headed. It replaces the meditation's first criterion, made out of — which sorts the cases backward, since the chick is drawn from the egg's matter more dramatically than the table is from the tree's — with the criterion that does the work: derivation across two numerically distinct subjects, as against traversal through the phases of one. It then finds that "comes from" equivocates even in the chicken, between the egg a hen was and the egg a hen lays, so that the identity holds of development while the generational regress must be met another way, and met modestly: the lineage discloses no privileged seam at which a first chicken announces itself, though every individual chicken begins. Turned on the self, "before me" divides into three questions wearing one grammar: whether an earlier self stands behind this one — none does, for a continuant has earlier phases but no earlier self; whether a first source stands behind this life — none does, for ancestry is a convergence no one antecedent completes, opening onto the Ground that is no member of it; and whether the self began at all — which it did, not by an earlier self but by a beginning, the coming-to-be of the one for whom a before is afterward possible. In the human case, on a reading marked as doctrinal, that beginning includes a mode of coming-to-be the series cannot hand forward, the immediately created rational soul — while body and soul constitute one human nature, not a soul lodged in a body. The title's two words name no two things: me is the person under the question of persistence, myself the same person under the question of origin, and of each there is no before — the one because it cannot be two along a line, the other because its origin is a beginning and not an earlier term.

<br>

## I. The Meditation and Its Two Pictures

Set two sentences of derivation side by side and listen for the difference. The table came from the tree. The chicken came from the egg. The grammar is the same; the metaphysics is not, and the whole essay lives in the gap the shared grammar hides.

Of the table it is simply true that the tree was before it. The tree stood, was felled, was cut, and out of its wood a table was made — and when the table stands there is no tree, only wood that was the tree's, wearing a shape the tree never wore. Two things, and an order between them: the tree first, the table after, the one made out of the other. Ask what was before the table and the answer is an earlier thing, a different thing, a thing that is not the table and is gone.

Of the chicken the same words say something else. The chicken came from the egg — but the egg it came from did not stand beside it, hand it something, and remain. The egg became the chicken, as the boy became the man: one living thing passing through its own phases, not one thing set aside while another is built from its parts. Ask which came first and you have asked which came first, the boy or the man — which is to ask what was before yourself.

That is the meditation, and it is right about one thing and imprecise about two others. It is right that the chicken-question, as the riddle poses it, treats a single continuant as a pair of relata and then demands their order, as one might demand whether a man's childhood or his age is the senior — a question with the form of a question and the content of a confusion. It is imprecise, first, about what makes the chicken the egg, for the mark it reaches for is not the mark that does the work; and imprecise, second, about how much the identity settles, for the chicken-question hides a second "before" that identity does not touch. And it points, the whole while, past the chicken, to the first person — where a third "before" waits that neither the chicken nor the table can teach us to answer.

<br>

## II. Made Out Of, or Grown Into

What separates the table from the chicken? The tempting answer is the meditation's: the table is made out of the tree, and the chicken is not made out of the egg but is it. This fails as stated, and the true criterion is hiding directly behind the false one.

It fails because the chick is made out of the egg — more dramatically, if anything, than the table is made out of the tree. Its growing body is built from what the egg supplies — yolk and albumen furnishing the resources of its development — while the wood of the table is more nearly the same matter unchanged than the chick is the same matter as what fed it. Material derivation, taken as the mark, would put the chicken in the table's column twice over. So matter is not the hinge.

The hinge is number. Two definitions, in place of the one the meditation reached for. Derivation is succession across numerically distinct subjects: one thing gives rise to another thing, and there are two. Traversal is the succession of phases within one numerically continuous subject: one thing passes through its own states, and there is only ever one. The table is a derivation — tree and table are two — and the ceasing of the tree, though real, is incidental to the count; what makes the tree before the table is simply that the table is a second subject, not that the first died. The chicken is a traversal — and here is all that "the chicken is the egg" was ever entitled to mean: not that the hatched bird is identical with shell and yolk and white, but that the organism developing within the fertilized egg is numerically the same organism that later hatches, with no second subject intervening between them. Once that is said, the provocation may stand: the egg is the chicken, not yet.

Put the criterion in a sentence and it corrects the meditation without discarding it. What was before it asks for an earlier thing, and receives one, exactly when the transition crosses from one subject to a second; and it asks for an earlier thing and receives none exactly when the transition runs through the phases of a single subject that endures. The table has a before because the tree was a second thing. The chicken has no before of that kind because the egg was not a second thing. The meditation was right that the chicken is the egg; it was only wrong about what makes that so — and the mistake matters, because "made out of" would have handed the chicken to the wrong column at the first push.

<br>

## III. The Two "Comes From"

Grant the correction and a harder difficulty surfaces, for the word that misled us about the table now misleads us about the chicken from a second direction. Every chicken comes from an egg; every egg comes from a chicken. The riddle's whole motive force is in the second clause, and the second clause is not about the chicken's own egg.

The egg a chicken comes from and the egg a chicken comes to lay are not the same egg, and the chicken on each side is not the same chicken. The egg this hen hatched from was the hen, not yet — identity, traversal, the thing the last section secured. The egg this hen lays is not the hen; it is her offspring, not yet — a second continuant, as distinct from her as any child from any parent. So "comes from" is doing two jobs in the one sentence that states the paradox: the developmental "comes from," a being's relation to its own earlier phase, which is identity; and the reproductive "comes from," a being's relation to the distinct being that produced it — succession across two numerically distinct subjects, the very count the table showed, differing only in that the parent does not cease when the offspring begins.

So the identity-move answers only one half. "The chicken is the egg" is true of the developmental "comes from" and silent about the reproductive one — and the reproductive one is where the regress lives, for each chicken is laid by a prior chicken and lays a later one. Here the corpus's earlier essay did the work: the generational line is a continuum, and across it nature marks off no stage as the first chicken — not because no organism ever first satisfied whatever definition we please to draw, but because the boundary a "first" would occupy is a line drawn for naming and not a seam in the thing. The claim is the narrower and surer one: the lineage discloses no ontologically privileged seam at which a first chicken announces itself. The individual organism, for its part, has a determinate biological beginning, associated with fertilization; what it lacks is a predecessor of itself, an earlier chicken that was this chicken. Earlier chickens, yes; an earlier self, no. The distinction the chicken is about to lend the first person is already here, in miniature: the identity-move does not replace the continuum argument; it completes the other half, and hands forward the one distinction the self will need.

<br>

## IV. The Convergence Behind "Before Me"

Now the question turns where it was always going, for no one has ever truly lain awake over the chicken. What was before me? The sentence inherits both of the chicken's "comes from," and a third besides; take the generational one first, since the chicken has already taught us to answer it.

Read as the reproductive clause, "what was before me" asks after my origin along the line of descent: my parents, and theirs, the lineage that produced this body. *Whose Condensation Am I?* pressed that line back through father and mother, each an unranked convergence in turn, and found no one ancestor who is the complete source of what I am — only more convergence, a net and not a chain. Note exactly what the net proves and what it does not. It does not, by its branching alone, prove that the regress has no earliest nodes; a net may have several roots. It proves the narrower thing, and the sufficient one: that no single antecedent is the privileged and complete source of the descendant. The rest — that no member of the nexus, earliest or otherwise, accounts for the actuality of the nexus at all — is the work not of topology but of the Ground.

Here Ground means God as the non-derivative source upon which the series depends, not the earliest member of the series; and it is the Ground, not a first ancestor, that the ancestral "before me" finally opens onto. For a first ancestor, however distant, would be one more member of the net — and the net was what wanted accounting for. The first of the three questions is answered, then, and answered by a prior essay: no single thing along the line of descent is the complete source of me, because the line is a convergence, and a convergence opens not onto its oldest member but onto what is no member of it.

<br>

## V. "Before Myself" Has No Answer That Is a Self

The second question is the developmental one, and here the meditation's own instinct is exactly right. Read this way, "what was before me" asks whether an earlier self stands behind this present self — whether the one I now am replaced some prior one I used to be, the way the table replaced the tree.

It did not, and cannot, and the reason is the whole burden of *The Infinite Interior*. I am not a series of selves handed forward one to the next across the seams between them; I am one continuant, crossing the interior of my own changing without a boundary at which an earlier me ended and a later me began. The child I was is not a former occupant of this life whom I succeeded; he is this life, younger — the egg to my chicken, the boy to the man. So the continuant has earlier phases, but no earlier self. To ask what self was before myself is to ask the chicken-question in the first person and in its purest form, stripped even of the generational ambiguity, for here there is only the one subject and no second anywhere in view. And the question dissolves: "before myself" would require two of me on one line, an earlier self for the later to come after, and a continuant is precisely what refuses to be two. I am not an item in my own succession of befores; I am the one whose succession it is.

<br>

## VI. The Ground Who Is Not the First Member

Before the third question, clear a confusion that has been gathering, for "no first" has now been said of three different things, and the corpus's discipline is never to let one phrase carry three verdicts. The convergence has no single privileged source, because it is a net. The continuant has earlier phases but no earlier self, because it is one traversal. And the Ground is no member of any series at all — not the earliest term of time, as it is not the largest region of space — because a first member would be one more thing within the order, and the Ground is what the order depends on. "No first," then, names not one condition but three different failures of the question "what came first?" to apply: no privileged source, no earlier self, no member to be first among.

The temptation is to hear the three as one, and to let the self's not-being-a-member rhyme with the Ground's into a likeness of status. The rhyme is real and the likeness is not. The self is not one member among a succession of numerically distinct selves, in the way a continuant is not a seam in itself — a fact about the shape of a received and sustained thing. The Ground is not one member among derivative beings, as the non-derivative is never a term among derivatives. The one is held out of the series; the other holds the series.

<br>

## VII. The Beginning That Is Not a Before

Now the third question, and the turn the whole essay was built toward, for here the human case parts from the chicken's — at exactly the point where the meditation, left to its momentum, would have fused them.

Ask whether the chicken, as such, had a beginning. The individual organism did — a determinate biological beginning, and nothing here denies it. The chicken is Ground-sustained, as everything that is; so the difference the essay is after cannot be that the chicken receives nothing from beyond the series, for it does, as every creature does. The difference lies within generation. Within its biological generation, there is no counterpart to the human case considered below: no principle whose coming-to-be is held, on the doctrinal reading, to be immediate rather than generated through the parents. (This retires a sentence of the earlier essay that overreached: the chicken is not "wholly self-inheritance," for convergence always counted the uninherited rain as well as the inherited seed. The chicken is inheritance and reception both.)

The human body is no different: a traversal like any organism, condensed de limo terrae across the same long becoming, with no biological seam in the lineage that, by itself, identifies the coming-to-be of the first human person. If "before me" meant only the body and its descent, the human answer would be the chicken's exactly.

The philosophical argument reaches only this far: the origin of the subject cannot be explained merely by indefinitely extending the subject's own inheritance backward. What follows names, under the doctrinal reading of this corpus, how the human case is understood at that limit.

On the reading this corpus takes — marked here as a doctrinal reading and not a demonstrated one — the human person's origin includes, with respect to the rational soul, a mode of coming-to-be the parents do not supply and the series cannot hand forward: the rational soul is not drawn from an earlier soul but immediately created by God as the form of this one human being.

Body and soul are not two natures here but one; the person is not a soul lodged in a body but a single embodied subject. What is said is not that a soul arrives into an already-standing body as a part, but that the subject who begins — body and soul, one nature — begins with respect to his rational soul in a way no biological inheritance accounts for.

And this is the hinge. Null self-inheritance — the corpus's term — means precisely this and only this: at the originating actuality of the self, there is no earlier state of this same self from which the self as such is inherited. It does not mean that nothing precedes the person causally; ancestry, matter, a whole world precede it. Inheritance can account for what a subject carries forward once the subject is underway; it cannot, merely by being run backward, account for the coming-to-be of the subject whose inheritance it is. That is the proposition the chicken could not teach alone and the first person makes unavoidable.

So the deepest "before" is answered by a beginning and not by an earlier thing — and the distinction is the whole essay. A before is an earlier term in a series: the tree before the table, the ancestor along the line, the phase before the phase. A beginning is not an earlier term but the positing of the term — the coming-to-be of a subject for whom there can afterward be a before at all. The creature so begun really begins, and begins in time: its first actuality has a date and a place in the order. What is not a member of its series is not the creature's beginning but the cause by which it is given — God, who is not an earlier item in the creature's history but the giving of the line. To have an origin that is not an earlier self does not mean the self stands outside time; it means that no earlier self, and no earlier member of any kind, is what its beginning consists in.

Just here the beginning makes its one analogical approach to the Ground, which must be measured and let go. The soul's beginning is not reached by running a series back to an earlier thing; neither is the Ground. There the likeness ends. The soul begins; the Ground does not. The soul's not-being-a-before is received and given; the Ground's is owed to nothing. What they share is the shape of the negation, and nothing else — and everything else is the distance the corpus exists to keep.

<br>

## VIII. Who, Then, Was Before Me, or Myself?

The riddle about poultry can now be answered in the first person, and the answer is three, because "before me" was three questions in one grammar. Along the line of descent, no single thing was the complete source of me, for what is there is a convergence no one ancestor completes, opening at last onto the Ground that is no member of it. Along the interior of my own life, no earlier self was before me, for I am one continuant and not a relay: I had earlier phases, I had antecedents, I did not have an earlier self. And at the origin of the self as such, nothing was before me in the way an earlier thing is before a later — yet I am not therefore beginningless, for I had a beginning, which is not another me standing before me but the positing of the one who would ask.

The title's two words are not two things. They are one person under two questions. Me is the question of persistence — the self traced through its phases — and of the me there is no earlier self, because a continuant cannot be two along one line. Myself is the question of origin — the coming-to-be of the one who persists — and of the myself there is no before either, because its origin is a beginning and not an earlier term. Same subject; two inquiries; and only a being who is both the asking continuant and the begun subject could have posed the question at all. The chicken supplied the developmental problem — earlier phase, not earlier self — but not the human question, the immediate origin of the rational soul. It was the question's occasion and never its subject.

<br>

## IX. Coda

The table has a tree before it, can point to it, and the tree is gone. The chicken can point two ways and must not confuse them: along its own development, back only to earlier states of itself; along its descent, to distinct progenitors who are not it. And the man — who is both the chicken's kind of becoming and something the chicken is not — can point along his descent and find no single source, and along his life and find no earlier self, and still not be a thing without an origin, because at the root of the one question that is neither descent nor development there is a beginning that is not a before: the giving of the one who could afterward ask.

The Scripture that says Before Abraham was, I am does not place the speaker earlier in the line than Abraham, a more ancient member of one succession. Held to the method of this essay — as metaphysics, and not as the establishing of a creed — it marks the limit the whole argument has traced: that the deepest answer to the question of origin is not an earlier thing, but a mode of being the word before cannot reach. The metaphysics can show why "before" does not exhaust that "I am"; it cannot, of itself, say what the "I am" means — that belongs to another reading, under another warrant. What this essay can say is only its own: that the creature made to ask "what was before myself?" is answered, in the end, not by an earlier self, but by the One from whom its beginning, and every beginning, is given.
