## Note on Sources and Method

This essay asks the question the corpus has been circling without naming. Earlier essays asked why there is this subject at all, why it is actual, how its earlier and later phases are one, and what the person does within the becoming he cannot refuse. The present question is more primitive than any of these: not why things change, nor how a self survives change, but what must be true for us to say, literally, that something has changed. By mutatio this essay means what is asserted when we say that something has changed: the becoming-otherwise of a continuant. It does not attempt here a theory of coming-to-be or passing-away, where the question is precisely whether the same subject stands on both sides of the transition. It presupposes rather than re-derives four prior essays: [*Who Was Before Me, or Myself?*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophical-theology/philosophy-of-mind/thomas-aquinas/2026/10/17/who-was-before-me-or-myself-en.html), for the distinction between derivation and traversal; [*The Infinite Interior*](https://oscargaitan.org/ontology/philosophy-of-time/2026/05/05/the-infinite-interior.html), for continuity as an unbroken interior crossed without a seam; [*The Self That Must Traverse*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophical-anthropology/philosophy-of-mind/philosophical-theology/2026/10/18/the-self-that-must-traverse-en.html), for the restriction that traversal is the mode of persistence and not the subject; and [*To Become Otherwise*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/philosophical-anthropology/philosophy-of-religion/2026/10/10/to-become-otherwise-en.html), for interior change and the reception by which a subject is otherwise without becoming another. It draws, where it reaches the Ground, on the essays of the temporal and grounding sequence. Where the argument touches the subsistence of being beneath becoming, it marks, as the corpus always marks, the line between a philosophical proposition and a doctrinal reading, and claims only the weaker and surer form. Aristotle, Aquinas, Heraclitus, and the voice behind Eppur si muove are engaged as company on the road, not authorities from which the argument descends.

<br>

## Abstract

Change is not the enemy of identity; it is a predicate that presupposes it. To say that something changed is to say three things at once that no two of them can supply: that there is a difference, that there is one subject, and that the difference has become actual in that one subject across an unbroken transition. Difference without one subject is mere succession; one subject without difference is mere rest; and succession without continuity is replacement, in which nothing survives to have changed. The essay states the positive thesis the corpus has approached since The Infinite Interior: change is the becoming-otherwise of one numerically continuous subject — the same, otherwise. Actuality explains how the otherwise becomes real; continuity explains why this is not replacement; traversal names the relation between the phases; the subject names whose change it is; and the Ground explains why none of this is self-sustaining. For change presupposes being: becoming does not produce the subject of which it is predicated. The point sharpens an old anxiety about machines. An algorithmic state transition does not, by itself, establish an ontologically continuous subject of change; an update describes what happens between representations, while mutatio asks, additionally, what — if anything — remains the numerically identical subject of those differences. The verb moves what is predicated of the subject; it does not exchange the subject for the predicate's successor. Eppur si muove.

<br>

## I. Eppur si muove

The phrase survives for its defiance, but its philosophy lies in something Italian leaves unspoken. Legend puts it under Galileo's breath after the recantation — "and yet it moves" — and the courage is real; but the decisive element is not muove, and, strictly, it is not si either. In si muove the si is the clitic of a pronominal construction, not a subject; Italian lets the subject go unsaid. The subject is there nonetheless, and it is the Earth. English cannot leave it unsaid and must restore it: "and yet it moves." That unspoken "it" is the whole of the matter. For there to be a movement that is its movement, there must already be something whose movement it is — and here the grammar makes visible a structure the ontology requires: the predicate presupposes something of which it is predicated. A verb of change needs a subject before it can predicate anything of it, and the subject it needs is not produced by the predicating. "It moves" is not the report of two things, a stillness replaced by a motion; it is the report of one thing of which motion is now true. Strip the "it" and "moves" has nothing left to be the movement of. The earth does not move because there is motion in the world; this motion is in the world because the earth, one body, is now where it was not. Eppur si muove says, before it says anything about astronomy, that something stays to do the moving.

<br>

## II. Difference Is Not Yet Change

Set two facts side by side. A thing was here. A second thing is there. I have described a difference — here is not there — and a succession — was is before is. I have not yet described a change. Difference is a relation between two descriptions; succession is an order between two times; and the pair of them, laid end to end, gives at most a world with variety in it, not a world in which anything has altered. A red tile and, beside it, a blue one differ without either changing. Noon and, after it, midnight succeed without the day having passed from the one to the other, unless there is a day to which both belong. What the pair lacks is the one thing a change requires and a mere difference-in-succession does not supply: a single bearer of whom both the earlier and the later are true. Until that bearer is named, "was here, is there" is a catalogue of two states, not the history of one.

<br>

## III. Replacement Is Not Change

Add the bearer wrongly and the catalogue is still not a change. Suppose the thing that was here is annihilated, and a thing exactly like it is created there. Now there is a difference, a succession, and — in the loosest sense — a "bearer" at each end. Yet nothing has changed in the strict sense at issue here, because nothing has survived as the numerically identical subject of the change. This is the structure the corpus named derivation in *Who Was Before Me, or Myself?*: a crossing from one numerically distinct subject to another, in which numerical identity does not cross the arrow. Write it A → B. The table comes from the tree so; the second subject begins where the first leaves off, and the first is in no way obliged to become the second because it never becomes anything — it ends, and another begins. *The Infinite Interior* fixed the geometry of the difference: replacement requires a boundary, a seam at which one thing stops and another starts, whereas genuine transition crosses an unbroken interior and admits no such seam. Where there is a seam, there are two; and where there are two, the later has not changed, for it never was the earlier. Replacement is the maximal difference and the minimal change: everything is otherwise, and nothing has altered.

<br>

## IV. The Subject of Change

So the question "what changes?" is harder than it looks, for three tempting answers all fail. It cannot be the changing that changes: that is circular, naming the event as its own subject. It cannot be a sequence of states: states do not change themselves; each merely is what it is, and their sequence is one more succession awaiting a bearer. It cannot be the difference between before and after: the difference is a relation between descriptions and supplies no one for the descriptions to be descriptions of. The answer that remains is the only one that is not a disguise for one of these: a subject changes. And subject must be taken here in its broad sense — not a conscious person, but that of which the before and the after are truly predicated, that which was otherwise and is otherwise and is, through both, one. The tree grows. The stone warms. The chicken matures. The person changes his mind. Four kinds of change, one grammar: something becomes otherwise, and the something is not a fourth description added to the earlier and the later, but the one they are both predicated of.

<br>

## V. Continuity

What binds the earlier determination and the later into states of one subject, rather than facts about two? Not their likeness — a perfect copy is maximally like and numerically other. Not their succession — succession orders replacements as readily as changes. The binding is continuity — not mere temporal connectedness, nor resemblance between successive states, but the uninterrupted persistence of the one subject through the difference — and *The Infinite Interior* supplied its form: the two determinations belong to one subject because the subject crosses from the one to the other along an unbroken interior, without a boundary at which it stops being itself. Write the change, pedagogically, A₁ → A₂, where the subscripts mark phases or determinations of one A and the arrow is not a seam but a passage. The notation of replacement, A → B, lets numerical identity fall away across the arrow; the notation of change does not. One should prefer the subscripts to the prime of A → A′, because the prime can suggest, against its intention, a derived second object, a near-copy — exactly the replacement the account is built to exclude.

<br>

## VI. Actualization

Only now, with the subject and its continuity in place, is it safe to let actuality speak — and the order matters. An earlier formulation in this corpus's workshop read: Mutatio est actualizatio alterius possibilitatis in eodem subiecto — change is the actualization of another possibility in the same subject. It is close, and it is not the primary definition, because it makes possibility do too much work too early. Put first, it makes change sound like selection from a standing menu of possible states, as though the subject reached into a catalogue and drew one out. The corpus is subtler: possibility is plural, actuality singular — but the decisive fact in mutatio is not first that one possibility wins. It is that a difference becomes actual in the very subject to which the earlier determination belonged. So actuality is placed second, not first. This does not reject the Aristotelian account of change; it asks a prior question about the subject to which the actualization belongs. The primary definition is simpler, and already given: mutatio is the becoming-otherwise of the same. Otherwise names a changed determination, not a numerically different subject. Actualization then explains how the otherwise becomes actual; continuity explains why this is not replacement; traversal names the relation between the phases; the subject names whose change it is.

<br>

## VII. The Modes of Mutatio

One formal structure, several operations. The stone warms: a physical alteration, a determination actualized in a body without the kind of reception characteristic of living or personal change. The tree grows and the chicken matures: living traversal, which *The Self That Must Traverse* marked off as the temporal mode in which a living, embodied continuant persists — becoming otherwise along its phases because to go on living at all is to go on as changed. The person changes his mind: interior change, which *To Become Otherwise* isolated and gave its operation — reception, by which a subject takes in what did not simply follow from its inheritance and is differently constituted while remaining the one who received. These are not one operation under three names. Warming is undergone without answer; living traversal is undergone as the course of a life; reception is undergone and answered. But they are instances of one form: in each, a difference becomes actual in a numerically continuous subject, which is otherwise and is not another. The grammar does not thin as it rises from stone to soul; it deepens, because what the subject is, and what it can do at the crossing, changes — while that it is one across the change does not.

<br>

## VIII. The Algorithmic Test

The sharpest use of the account is where the age is most confused. It is tempting, and too strong, to say that a machine cannot change — that a database taking in new information merely updates and does not alter. A computer undergoes perfectly ordinary physical change: its memory cells change electrical state, its media are modified, its temperature climbs under load. Nothing in this ontology needs to deny it. The sharper claim is this: an algorithmic state transition does not, by itself, establish an ontologically continuous subject of change. state₁ → state₂ → state₃ reports an ordered sequence; it does not report what the bearer of those states is. Perhaps one persistent physical substrate is being altered. Perhaps one model instance is destroyed and another instantiated, a seam dressed as a passage. Perhaps "version 2" is only a conventionally related successor, A → B renamed A₁ → A₂. The transition notation alone cannot decide among these. So the modern question is exact at last: an update describes what happens between representations; mutatio requires us to ask, additionally, what — if anything — remains the numerically identical subject of those differences. The old anxiety about algorithmic replacement was not wrong; it was imprecise. The danger is not that the machine changes too much, but that where we read change there may be only succession across a seam, difference with no one to whom it belongs.

<br>

## IX. Change Is Not Ground

There is a last thing the account can now say that its neighbors approached and did not quite state. Change presupposes being — not in time, as though the subject first sat motionless and afterward began to change, but in the order of being itself. There can be no becoming unless there is something whose becoming it is. *The Self That Must Traverse* refused to identify the subject with its traversal: becoming is the mode, not the ground; the self traverses, but the traversing does not sustain the self. The present essay can therefore draw the more limited consequence that mutatio does not produce the subject of which it is predicated — it presupposes it. The alteration belongs to the predicate; the continuity belongs to the subject. And since the continuous subject does not hold itself in being merely by continuing — a thing's going-on is not its ground, as the temporal sequence of this corpus argued and [*Two Meanings of 'Is'*](https://oscargaitan.org/ontology/philosophy-of-time/2026/04/21/two-meanings-of-is.html) sharpened — the subject of change is itself a received being, held actual by what its own becoming cannot supply: the non-derivative I AM WHO I AM, the Ground that is not a member of the series it sustains. Here the essay marks its line, as doctrine and not demonstration, and claims only this: that becoming, which cannot found the one who becomes, points past itself to a ground that does not become.

<br><br>

## X. Eppur si muove — Revisited

Return, then, to the small defiant sentence, and hear what it was saying beneath the astronomy. It does not say that movement exists, as though motion were a thing loose in the world. It says that it moves. The verb alters what is predicated of the subject; it does not exchange the subject for the predicate's successor. Something was otherwise. Something is otherwise. The "something" is one — and because it is one, the two "otherwise"s are not two facts but one history. This is the phrase that holds the whole: not the same despite change, as though identity and change were enemies made to tolerate each other; not the same underneath change, as though change were a surface over a motionless depth; but the same, otherwise. The difference is real. The sameness is real. Neither is bought by denying the other. Since *The Infinite Interior* the corpus has refused both the temptation to turn persistence into serial replacement and the temptation to secure identity by freezing what persists; the positive thesis can now be stated plainly. Mutatio is not what threatens numerical identity. Mutatio is what can be predicated at all only because numerical identity gives the difference one subject to belong to.

> Quid est mutatio?
> The same, otherwise.
> Eppur si muove.
