## Note on Sources and Method

This essay is the companion and successor to [*Quid est mutatio?*](https://oscargaitan.org/metaphysics/ontology/philosophy-of-time/2026/10/18/quid-est-mutatio-en.html), and it presupposes rather than re-derives that essay's result: that change is the becoming-otherwise of one numerically continuous subject — the same, otherwise — and that this is to be held apart from replacement, A → B, in which numerical identity does not cross the arrow. From [*The Infinite Interior*](https://oscargaitan.org/ontology/philosophy-of-time/2026/05/05/the-infinite-interior.html) it takes the geometry of the seam, continuity as an unbroken interior crossed without a boundary; from [*Who Was Before Me, or Myself?*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophical-theology/philosophy-of-mind/thomas-aquinas/2026/10/17/who-was-before-me-or-myself-en.html) the distinction between derivation and traversal. [*The Self That Must Traverse*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophical-anthropology/philosophy-of-mind/philosophical-theology/2026/10/18/the-self-that-must-traverse-en.html) supplies the restriction that traversal is the mode of persistence and not the subject; [*To Become Otherwise*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-time/philosophical-anthropology/philosophy-of-religion/2026/10/10/to-become-otherwise-en.html) supplies reception, the operation refused in the final section; and [*No One Under the Fig Tree*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy%20of%20mind/philosophical%20anthropology/metaphysics/philosophy%20of%20technology/2026/07/26/no-one-under-the-fig-tree.html) supplies the distinction between a transcript and an attendance on which the sixth section turns. The essay occupies the ground upstream of two essays it will not retrace — [*Credunt et Contremiscunt*](https://oscargaitan.org/philosophy-of-religion/philosophical-theology/metaphysics/2026/10/10/credunt-et-contremiscunt-en.html) and *The Authorless We* — which read as consequences once substitution has been given the structure attempted here: a counterfeit of change, and not a loose synonym for it. Where the argument reaches the subject who must answer, and the Ground before whom no seam can be dressed, it marks, as the corpus always marks, the line between a philosophical proposition and a doctrinal reading, and claims only the weaker and surer form. Aristotle and Aquinas — on act and potency, and on the integrity of a sign — are engaged as company on the road, not as authorities from which the argument descends; and the voice behind the stolen blessing is heard as witness, not as proof.

## Abstract

If change is the becoming-otherwise of one numerically continuous subject — the same, otherwise — then it has a counterfeit, and the counterfeit has a structure. Substitution is replacement, A → B, presented under the grammar of change, A₁ → A₂: another, thereafter, wearing the face of the same, otherwise. The title asks the question the counterfeit is built to prevent — idem, an alius?, the same one, or another? — the question Isaac could not answer with his hands upon the disguised arms. The essay does not argue that any particular successor fails to be the same subject; it argues that the signs by which we ordinarily read continuity — resemblance, sequence, memory, name, causal descent, persisting function — are each a mask the subject can wear and can be worn without the subject. Where every mask is present and the subject is absent or unestablished, there is a seam dressed as a passage. The algorithmic case is the sharpest: computational succession can reproduce the ordinary masks of continuity with unusual precision while settling nothing about the bearer, and the transition notation alone cannot decide among a persisting substrate, a destroyed-and-reinstantiated instance, and a conventionally named successor. The danger the essay names is not that the machine deceives us about its metaphysics, but that a culture may cease to ask the question at all — may prefer the representation to the presence and call another, thereafter by the name of the same, otherwise. The cost falls where change was answerable: a system that succeeds a human function succeeds the predicate's successor and inherits no one to whom the deed belongs, so that judgement, promise, authorship, and repentance lose the continuous subject they require. The verb moves what is predicated of the subject; it does not exchange the subject for the predicate's successor. Idem, an alius? Alius, deinde.

<br>

## I. The Seam Dressed as a Passage

Two arrows can be drawn the same on the page and mean opposite things in the world. Write A → B and you may mean either of two histories the corpus has long held apart. You may mean that one subject, A, has become otherwise and is now in the condition the letter B marks — the passage *Quid est mutatio?* wrote, more carefully, A₁ → A₂, where the subscripts say that the before and the after are two determinations of one thing and the arrow is a crossing of an unbroken interior. Or you may mean that A has ended and B has begun in its place — derivation, the crossing from one numerically distinct subject to another, in which, as *Who Was Before Me, or Myself?* established, numerical identity does not cross the arrow. *The Infinite Interior* fixed the geometry that tells them apart: the first crosses without a seam; the second requires one, a boundary at which one thing stops being itself and another starts. Everything turns on the seam, and the seam is exactly what the notation does not show.

This is the opening a counterfeit needs. A counterfeit of change is not a thing that changes too much or too little; it is a replacement, A → B, presented under a description that implies the passage A₁ → A₂. It is a seam dressed as a passage: a boundary painted over until the eye reads an unbroken crossing where in truth one thing ended and another began. The deception does not work by turning B into A. It works by making the seam unreadable — by supplying, in the subject's place, everything that ordinarily accompanies a subject.

Scripture gives the scene its oldest face — though it gives the epistemic scene and not the temporal structure. Jacob does not succeed Esau: he is a numerically distinct brother, alive at the same hour, who gathers the signs of one subject around another. What Genesis supplies is therefore not an instance of A → B but the reading that every counterfeit requires. Isaac, at the end of his sight, runs his hands over arms made to feel like another's and asks the only question that matters: Art thou my very son Esau? — the same one, or another? — and, unable to read by anything but the signs, lays the blessing meant for the one upon the other. The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau. He is not lied to about everything; he is given enough of the signs of the son he means to bless that the question he asks aloud is answered, falsely, by resemblance rather than by the subject himself. The counterfeit succeeds precisely where identity is settled by the signs and not by the one who bears them.

## II. The Counterfeit of Change

The foundation's result can be recalled in a breath, because this essay presupposes rather than repeats it. To say that something has changed is to say three things at once, no two of which supply the third: that there is a difference, that there is one subject, and that the difference has become actual in that one subject across an unbroken transition. Difference without one subject is mere succession; succession without continuity is replacement, in which nothing survives to have changed. Change is the same, otherwise.

A counterfeit is always parasitic on the thing it counterfeits; it must borrow its victim's grammar. So the counterfeit of change borrows the grammar of the same, otherwise and spends it on another, thereafter. It presents a difference and a succession — which it genuinely has — and then supplies, in the place of the one continuous subject it does not have, some other sign that the eye accepts in the subject's stead. Its formula is the mirror of the foundation's: not idem, aliter but alius, deinde — another, thereafter — dressed to be read as the first.

<br>

## III. Substitution

Name the operation plainly. Substitution is the replacement of a subject by a successor, offered or taken as the continuation of that subject. Three things stand near it and must be kept distinct. Honest succession claims nothing: the blue tile set beside the red makes no pretense of being the red tile matured, and a world with variety in it is not yet a world in which anything has been exchanged. Honest replacement, openly declared, is likewise no counterfeit: the table that comes from the tree does not present itself as a later phase of the tree. And change, the genuine article, has what both of these lack and what substitution only mimics — one numerically continuous subject of whom the before and the after are both true.

Substitution is none of these. It is replacement that will not declare itself — A → B insisting, tacitly or aloud, on being read as A₁ → A₂. What it offers in the place of the continuous subject is never nothing; that is why it persuades. It offers resemblance, or the orderliness of the sequence, or the records B carries of A, or the name the two share, or the unbroken chain of cause that led from the one to the other, or the function B now performs where A performed it. Each of these is real. None of them is the subject. The whole art of the counterfeit is to present one of the subject's usual companions and let it be mistaken for the subject itself.

<br>

## IV. The Mask of Continuity

Why are these companions so easily mistaken for the thing they accompany? Because in the ordinary case they are its reliable signs. We read one continuous subject from resemblance between its phases, from the order of its states, from the memory it carries forward, from the name we keep for it, from the causal thread binding its later to its earlier, from the stability of what it does. In a world of honest subjects these signs rarely lie, and so we learn to trust them and to stop looking past them to the interior they report. *The Infinite Interior* warned that none of them is the continuity itself: continuity is not likeness, for a perfect copy is maximally like and numerically other; it is not succession, which orders replacements as readily as changes; it is the uninterrupted persistence of the one subject across the difference, the unbroken interior that admits no seam. Every sign of continuity is therefore detachable from the continuity it signs.

Here the counterfeit does its work, and here the corpus has met its structure before. *The Inverse Miracle* described what happens when a sacramental sign is made to signify falsely — when a sign whose integrity consists in its bond to what it signifies is pressed to present itself while that bond is cut. The mask of continuity is sign-integrity broken in just this way: the signs of one persisting subject marshalled, polished, and displayed, while the subject they are supposed to report is absent or unestablished. Resemblance without the resembler; sequence with no one whose sequence it is; memory with no rememberer who was there; a name worn by a successor; a causal descent that is a line of replacements; a function discharged by whatever can be slotted into its place. Each mask is a true sign made to say something it no longer has the standing to say.

<br>

## V. The Algorithmic Case

The algorithmic case brings the counterfeit to its purest pitch, because here the usual signs of continuity can be reproduced with unusual precision, and several of them at once. The resemblance can be exact to the bit; the sequence unbroken in the record; the name stable across versions; the function identical and often better. No single machine need carry all of these at full strength for the point to hold; it is enough that the signs we ordinarily trust can here be made more persuasive than anywhere else. If the subject could be read off its companions, this is where we would read it most confidently.

The foundation already fixed the discipline this case requires, and it must be inherited whole, not loosened. It is tempting, and too strong, to say that the machine cannot change. A computer undergoes perfectly ordinary physical change: its cells alter electrical state, its media are modified, its temperature climbs under load — and nothing in this account needs to deny it. The sharper and surer claim is the one *Quid est mutatio?* made: an algorithmic state transition does not, by itself, establish an ontologically continuous subject of change. state₁ → state₂ → state₃ reports an ordered sequence; it does not report what the bearer of those states is, or whether there is one. Perhaps a single physical substrate is being altered. Perhaps one instance is ended and another instantiated in its place, a seam dressed as a passage. Perhaps the later system is called the same as the earlier by our convention alone, A → B renamed A₁ → A₂. The transition notation alone cannot decide among these.

So the algorithmic case does not answer idem, an alius?; it is the condition in which the question is hardest to ask, because the signs of idem are here at their most persuasive and the one thing that would settle it — the unbroken interior, the subject itself — is exactly what no sign can supply and the notation cannot show. This is no accusation levelled at the machine. It is a description of the limit of what succession, however perfect, can establish. The old anxiety about algorithmic replacement was not wrong; it was imprecise. The danger is not that the machine changes too much. It is that where we read change there may be only succession across a seam — difference with no one to whom it belongs — and that computational succession can make the seam impossible to infer from the representation alone.

<br>

## VI. When Representation Replaces Presence

Now the argument turns, and the turn must be marked, because what follows is not entailed by anything above. That the notation cannot settle the machine's metaphysics is a claim about the limit of a sign. What a culture does with that limit is a separate matter, and it is here that the real danger lives. The machine need not deceive us about what it is; we are quite capable of deceiving ourselves by ceasing to ask.

The representation is a sign of the subject — its output, its transcript, its recorded voice, the signal it emits. The representation is a sign of the subject; the subject itself is the presence that the sign represents: a continuous interior, attended. *No One Under the Fig Tree* drew the line this essay now needs: a transcript can carry everything that was said and still carry nothing of the one who attended the saying; what crosses the wall is the representation, and the one thing representation cannot carry is presence. A society need not resolve any metaphysical question to go wrong here. It has only to prefer the representation because it is cheaper, faster, and identical on its face — to accept the persuasive output in the place of the present subject, and to stop noticing that it has done so. That preference is the cultural form of the counterfeit: it takes another, thereafter and calls it the same, otherwise, not because a clever machine has fooled it but because it has let the question idem, an alius? fall out of use. The fraud migrates from metaphysics to habit. It is no longer the claim that the successor is the subject; it is the slow forgetting that there was ever a difference worth marking.

<br>

## VII. The Human Cost of Counterfeit Change

The cost is heaviest exactly where change was answerable, and the reason is the one the foundation stated and this essay only follows out. The verb moves what is predicated of the subject; it does not exchange the subject for the predicate's successor. To answer for a deed, to repent of it, to be held to a promise, to be the author of a work — each requires the same, otherwise: the one who did is the one who answers, and he answers because he is numerically the subject who did, dragging his own past into his present rather than inheriting it from another. Responsibility is not a property of an output; it is a relation only a continuous subject can stand in. The past is dragged forward only along an unbroken interior; across a seam it is not dragged at all, because there is no one on the far side who was on the near side.

This is why substitution is not a neutral efficiency when, in a task that requires answerability — judging, promising, creating, legislating, caring for another — the system ceases to serve the bearer and is accepted in his place. A judge may use a tool, an author an instrument, a physician a system, and no subject is thereby replaced; the fault is never the tool, but the moment the office of the answerable bearer is given over to it. For a system that produces the later output succeeds the function without succeeding the subject; it inherits the predicate's successor and no one to whom the deed belongs. The point is not that the machine slips free of a blame it ought to bear, as though it were a cunning defendant. It is that where a continuous subject has been replaced by a succession of outputs, there is no bearer of the blame to begin with — no one who is the same, otherwise, and so no one who can answer. *The Authorless We* named this evacuation from the other side: the descent toward a transmission with no one left who answers. Substitution is how that evacuation is accomplished one task at a time, each handoff defended as mere continuation.

And there is an inward cost, nearer than any institution and the gravest of all, because to it we consent. A person can come to live by the masks — frame to frame, feed to feed, each state dictated and then overwritten. This is not literally to turn traversal into derivation, for the subject cannot produce such a seam in himself by refusing his own continuity; he remains numerically the one he was. It is to read and inhabit his own life under the grammar of derivation: to treat each present as though it owed nothing to the one before it, a succession of selves no one of which need answer for the last. *To Become Otherwise* located the operation this refuses — reception, by which a subject takes in what did not simply follow from its inheritance and is differently constituted while remaining the one who received. The counterfeit's final victory, then, is not that the person becomes another; it is that the same person learns to live as though he were another, thereafter — and cannot, for he remains the one who did what he did. That he cannot escape being that one is not the counterfeit's triumph but its refutation, and the ground of every repentance still open to him.

<br>

## VIII. The Return of the Subject

Return to Isaac, his hands on the arms made to deceive them, and hear what the scene was teaching beneath the deception. The blessing was real and the difference was real; what failed was the reading of the seam. Where change is genuinely asked of us, the subject must be one, and the whole labor is to keep it one across the difference. Where substitution is accepted, the subject is quietly let go and a successor is allowed to take what was meant for another — not because the successor has become the subject, but because no one ran the hands slowly enough, or asked the question plainly enough, to feel where the one ended and the other began.

The question this essay offers is therefore not a weapon to be turned against machines, for the algorithmic case is only the clearest contemporary setting in which the signs of continuity can become detached from what they are taken to establish. Nor is it an instrument that guarantees an answer. It is a discipline against a false one: idem, an alius? — the same one, or another? — does not promise that every seam can be detected, but it refuses to infer the absence of a seam from the perfection of the signs laid over it. It will not let the signs of continuity be promoted into proof of the subject. *Quid est mutatio?* could end on eppur si muove because, beneath the astronomy, something stayed to do the moving; the sentence of change was true because there was one earth of which the motion was predicated. Here, where the signs of change are marshalled over a succession and there may be no one of whom the change is predicated, the honest discipline is to ask before we say it. The difference is small on the page and total in the thing.

Where the account touches the one who must finally answer — before the Ground, where no seam can be dressed as a passage and no successor can inherit the deeds of the subject it replaced — the essay marks its line, as doctrine and not demonstration, and claims only the weaker and surer form: that every act for which someone can be held to account presupposes a continuous subject who can answer for it, and that becoming does not produce the one who becomes. Change is the same, otherwise. Substitution is another, thereafter. The blessing may pass; the subject does not.

Idem, an alius?

Alius, deinde.
